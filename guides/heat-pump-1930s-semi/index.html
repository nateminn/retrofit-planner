Heat pump for a 1930s semi: cost and running costs

Updated 25 September 2026. Every figure on this page comes from our published model; see how accurate it is.

A typical 1930s semi is a 3 bed house, and many were built with cavity walls that have since been filled. With average insulation, a 1930s semi typically needs a heat pump of about 9 kW, falling to about 7.5 kW once it is well insulated. Installers recorded a typical cost of £11,000 to £15,500 for a heat pump of that size under the Boiler Upgrade Scheme, with a median of £12,700. After the £7,500 grant, most pay £3,500 to £8,000.

What it costs

Size, and so price, follows how much heat the home loses. These are the typical size and the recorded installed cost for a 1930s semi at each level of insulation. The installed cost is the whole job as installers reported it to the scheme: the heat pump, hot water cylinder, labour and VAT, before the grant. Some homes also need larger radiators, which can add to it; see radiator sizing.

What to check in a 1930s semi first

Three things decide how well a heat pump works in a house of this age. First, the walls: if the cavity has never been filled, filling it is one of the biggest cuts in heat loss you can make, a median 12.0 per cent less gas in homes the government measured. Second, the floor: many 1930s houses have suspended timber floors that are draughty, which underfloor insulation and draught-proofing address. Third, the radiators: rooms such as a bay-fronted lounge may need a larger radiator to stay warm at the lower flow temperatures heat pumps run at. An MCS installer's room by room heat loss survey shows which rooms need what.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a heat pump cost for a 1930s semi?

Typically £11,000 to £15,500 installed for a 9 kW heat pump, with a median of £12,700, from what installers recorded under the Boiler Upgrade Scheme in 2025/26. After the £7,500 grant that is £3,500 to £8,000, or £2,000 to £6,500 if you are replacing oil or LPG.

Is a heat pump cheaper to run than gas in a 1930s semi?

On an 18p heat pump tariff, yes: about £641 a year against £877 for gas with average insulation. On a standard electricity tariff it costs about £937, a little more than gas. Both figures are energy only; gas also carries a £108 a year standing charge you save if you cap the supply.

What size heat pump does a 1930s semi need?

About 9 kW with average insulation, and less once it is well insulated. The exact size comes from the room by room heat loss calculation an MCS installer must do. See our guide to heat pump sizes.
