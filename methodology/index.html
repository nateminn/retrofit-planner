Our Methodology

Figures last reviewed 23 September 2026. Energy prices are the Ofgem cap for 1 October to 31 December 2026 and change quarterly; the next cap is announced in late November.

Every number on Retrofit Planner comes from a published, verifiable source. Here's exactly how our calculators work, what data they use, and where we make assumptions.

Energy prices

We use Ofgem's quarterly price cap announcements as the basis for all energy cost calculations. Our current rates are from the price cap for 1 October to 31 December 2026, announced on 26 August 2026:

  • Electricity: 26.32p per kWh, standing charge 54.83p per day
  • Gas: 7.97p per kWh, standing charge 29.68p per day
  • Heating oil: 9.0p per kWh (September 2026 kerosene market price)
  • LPG: 9.5p per kWh (typical bulk contract)
  • Heat pump tariff: 18p per kWh effective, assuming a well-scheduled time-of-use tariff such as Octopus Cosy with around 60% of heating shifted into the cheap windows

We update our calculators when Ofgem announces each new price cap (usually late February, May, August and November), so the figures cover the coming quarter. We do not account for future price changes in our calculations because these are unpredictable. All estimates reflect current prices only.

Installation costs

Installation costs are sourced from the Energy Saving Trust (EST) and cross-referenced with MCS-certified installer quotes and trade body publications. We present cost ranges (min to max) rather than single figures because actual costs vary by property, location, and installer. Our "typical" cost used in payback calculations is the midpoint of the published range.

Heat pump calculations

Our heat pump calculator uses the following methodology:

  • Heat demand: from the DESNZ National Energy Efficiency Data-Framework (NEED) 2026, covering 2024 consumption in England and Wales. We took median gas consumption for every property type and floor area band from the 39,502 gas-heated records in the anonymised sample, fitted it against floor area for each type, then scaled it by bedroom count so the population weighted result reproduces NEED's own published medians by bedrooms (5,215 kWh for 1 bed up to 19,339 kWh for 5 or more). It matches to within 0.3% for 2 to 5 bedrooms. The heat pump calculator and the boiler comparison load this table directly from /js/heat-model.js, and the embeddable widget carries an identical copy inline. Guide pages do not run it: they state figures derived from it, and a check in the repository compares those figures against the table so they cannot drift unnoticed. The insulation, EPC and solar calculators model different things and are sourced separately, below.
  • Insulation adjustment: measured from the same records by comparing EPC bands within each property type and floor area cell, so that size is not counted twice. Across 17 such cells the medians relative to band D are A or B 0.62, C 0.85, D 1.00, E 1.11. That is a narrower spread than the rule of thumb most calculators use, and it is what the data supports.
  • Coefficient of Performance (COP): based on field data from the Electrification of Heat Demonstration Project. We use seasonal COP values of 2.6 to 3.4 depending on insulation level, which are conservative relative to manufacturer claims.
  • Grant deduction: the £7,500 Boiler Upgrade Scheme grant is applied to air source heat pump costs for eligible properties.
  • Running cost comparison: calculated as heat demand divided by COP, multiplied by the electricity rate, compared against heat demand divided by boiler efficiency (90% seasonal for a condensing gas boiler, below the figure on the badge) multiplied by the gas rate.

Insulation savings

Our insulation calculator uses Energy Saving Trust published typical savings data, broken down by property type and insulation measure. We adjust for heating fuel type using a multiplier based on relative fuel costs. The EST data represents median savings for each property type based on monitored installations.

Solar panel calculations

Our solar calculator uses:

  • Generation: PVGIS (Photovoltaic Geographical Information System) data from the European Commission Joint Research Centre, adjusted by UK region and roof orientation.
  • Self-use ratio: read from the MCS MGD 003 Issue 2.0 look-up tables, which set self-consumption from occupancy, annual electricity use and system generation rather than a single figure. A 4 kW array on a medium user who is out half the day self consumes about 20%, rising to about 55% with a 5 kWh battery.
  • Battery cost: £4,600 for a 5 kWh system, the Energy Saving Trust figure, added to the system cost when a battery is selected.
  • Smart Export Guarantee: we use 12p per kWh, the Outgoing Octopus rate, which is the most widely available flat SEG tariff. Rates checked on 20 September 2026 ran from 4.1p to 17.5p per kWh depending on supplier and tariff.
  • Panel degradation: 0.5% per year, which is the industry standard warranty assumption.

EPC calculations

Our EPC improvement planner estimates EPC point improvements based on BRE's Standard Assessment Procedure (SAP) methodology. We use typical point improvements per measure as published by accredited energy assessors and cross-referenced with EPC lodgement data. Actual EPC improvements vary by property, so our estimates should be treated as indicative.

Grant eligibility

Our grant checker uses published eligibility criteria from GOV.UK and scheme administrators. Grant schemes change frequently, so we review and update criteria monthly. However, we always recommend verifying eligibility directly with the scheme administrator before making decisions.

Limitations and disclaimers

Our calculators provide estimates based on national averages and published data. They are not substitutes for professional assessments. Key limitations include:

  • Actual costs vary by location, installer, property condition, and access difficulty.
  • Energy prices change quarterly and we cannot predict future prices.
  • EPC improvements depend on the specific property and assessor methodology.
  • Grant eligibility rules change and may vary by local authority area.
  • All calculations run in your browser. The figures you enter are not stored or transmitted; see our privacy policy for what the site does collect.

Always obtain professional quotes and assessments before proceeding with any installation.
