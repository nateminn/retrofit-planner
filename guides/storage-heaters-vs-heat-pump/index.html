Storage Heaters vs Heat Pump: Is It Worth Switching?

Replacing storage heaters with a heat pump saves £710 to £1,470 a year on a heat pump tariff in the four homes below. Storage heaters turn each unit of electricity into one unit of heat, mostly using cheaper night electricity. A heat pump delivers 2.55 to 3.05 units of heat for every unit of electricity in the middle half of homes measured in the Electrification of Heat trial, so each unit of heat costs about 47% less on a standard tariff and 63% less on a heat pump tariff. With the £7,500 Boiler Upgrade Scheme grant, the heat pump itself pays back in about 3 to 7 years in a 3-bed semi, before any new radiators and pipework.

Why storage heaters cost so much

Storage heaters charge overnight on a cheaper night-rate tariff (Economy 7 or Economy 10) and release heat during the day. That keeps their cost below direct electric heating, but several things still make them expensive:

They are only 100% efficient. Every 1 kWh of electricity produces 1 kWh of heat. A heat pump produced 2.55 to 3.05 kWh of heat per 1 kWh of electricity in the middle half of homes in the Electrification of Heat trial, so storage heaters use roughly two and a half to three times as much electricity for the same heat.

Daytime top-up is expensive. Many homes with storage heaters need extra heat in the late afternoon and evening when the stored heat runs out. On Economy 7 that top-up is charged at the day rate, about 32p/kWh on Octopus's rates, which is higher than the 26.32p single rate.

Poor control. You set the input the night before based on guessing tomorrow's weather. If it is warmer than expected, you waste energy. If it is colder, you need expensive daytime top-up. A heat pump adjusts in real time.

What the switch involves

Replacing storage heaters with a heat pump means installing a wet central heating system (radiators and pipework) if you do not already have one, and a hot water cylinder. This is the biggest difference from replacing a gas boiler, where the pipework already exists.

A home with no radiators needs a full set plus pipework, which costs more than the radiator changes in a gas boiler swap, so ask for it to be itemised in each quote.

At £1,111 a year saved (3-bed semi on a heat pump tariff), the heat pump pays back its £3,500 to £8,000 after the grant in about 3 to 7 years, or 4.7 years at the typical cost of £5,200. Every £1,000 of radiator and pipework work adds about 11 months.

Check for free funding first

If you are on a low income or certain benefits, ECO4 may help pay for the switch until it closes on 31 December 2026. In England, the Warm Homes: Local Grant, which replaced the Home Upgrade Grant, lets your council pay for agreed work, including air source heat pumps, in privately owned homes with an EPC of D to G where household income is usually £36,000 or less. ECO4 closes on 31 December 2026. The work has to be finished by then, not just applied for, and installers fill up as the date gets closer. Government has confirmed there will be no successor supplier obligation: from 2027 the money moves to council run schemes under the Warm Homes Plan, which you apply for through your local authority rather than your energy supplier. When to keep storage heaters

Small, well-insulated flat with no outdoor space. If you cannot fit a heat pump outdoor unit and your heating costs are low, modern high heat retention storage heaters, which hold their charge for longer, may be the practical choice. The Energy Saving Trust puts new storage heaters at £900 to £1,400 per room fitted. If your heaters are switched by a Radio Teleswitch (RTS) meter, that system is being phased out, so ask your supplier about a replacement meter.

You are renting. The landlord would need to fund the installation, and the grant goes to the owner. You can ask, but cannot force the change. The government has confirmed new minimum energy efficiency standards that privately rented homes must meet by 2030; see our guide to EPC minimum standards for landlords.

For most homes with outdoor space, the bigger the storage heater bill, the faster the payback: in our model, every £1,000 a year spent on storage heaters becomes about £365 on a heat pump tariff.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to replace storage heaters with a heat pump?

For a 3-bed semi, the heat pump costs £11,000 to £15,500 installed, or £3,500 to £8,000 after the £7,500 Boiler Upgrade Scheme grant. A home with storage heaters also needs radiators, pipework and a hot water cylinder, so ask for those to be itemised in each quote.

How much will I save?

About £710 to £1,470 a year on a heat pump tariff in our four example homes, and about £520 to £1,080 on a standard tariff. For a 3-bed semi the heat pump pays back in about 3 to 7 years after the grant, before any radiator work. See our running costs guide.

Can I get the switch for free?

Possibly. ECO4 may help until it closes on 31 December 2026, and in England the Warm Homes: Local Grant, which replaced the Home Upgrade Grant, pays for agreed work for eligible households through your council. Use our grant checker.
