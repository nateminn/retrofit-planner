EPC rating for a 1980s and 1990s home

The short answer

  • 74% of homes built between 1981 and 2002 are EPC band C or better, against 56% of all homes
  • 70 points on average, band C
  • Landlords need band C by 1 October 2030

Updated 25 September 2026. Every figure on this page comes from official statistics and our published model; see where our data comes from.

74% of homes in England built between 1981 and 2002 are EPC band C or better, against 56% of all homes. Their average rating is 70 points, band C, and 5% are band E or worse.

How 1980s and 1990s homes rate

Mostly band C, 70%. There are about 4.0 million of these homes in England; the table shows how their EPC bands compare with all homes.

Rules and grants

Landlords need band C by 1 October 2030. Check a rented home with the landlord EPC calculator.

ECO4 can fund insulation for households on qualifying benefits until 31 December 2026, and the Boiler Upgrade Scheme gives £7,500 off a heat pump. Read more about is cavity wall insulation worth it, how to improve an EPC rating, or see EPC ratings for every age of home.

How we worked this out

EPC bands and home features: English Housing Survey 2024, live tables DA7101, DA6201 and DA6101 (MHCLG, updated 9 July 2026), England. Gas use: NEED consumption tables 2026 (DESNZ), 2024 meter readings, England and Wales, whose age bands differ slightly from the survey's. The routes to band C are our simple EPC points model, the same as our EPC calculator, not the government's SAP software, for a home with loft insulation, double glazing and a gas boiler under 15 years old.

Frequently asked questions

What EPC rating does a 1980s and 1990s home usually have?

Band C on average: homes built between 1981 and 2002 average 70 points in the English Housing Survey 2024, against 68 for all homes. About 74% are band C or better, and 5% are band E or worse.

Can a 1980s and 1990s home reach EPC band C?

Yes: 74% of homes built between 1981 and 2002 are already band C or better. Our model puts a 1980s semi with unfilled cavity walls at about 70 points, band C: already band C or better.

Why do older homes have lower EPC ratings?

Walls are a big part of it. The rating models what a home costs to heat per square metre, and solid walls lose far more heat than filled cavity walls; 79% of homes built before 1919 have solid walls with no insulation.
