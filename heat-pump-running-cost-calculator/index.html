The electricity price where a heat pump wins

A heat pump is cheaper to run than your boiler when electricity costs less than the boiler's cost per kWh of heat multiplied by the heat pump's efficiency. Below these prices the heat pump wins; above them the boiler does.

The standard rate of 26.32p sits just above the gas break-even at median efficiency, which is why the tariff matters so much against gas. Against oil and LPG a heat pump is cheaper to run on any tariff under the price cap. Electric heating, whether storage heaters or panel heaters, turns 1 kWh of electricity into 1 kWh of heat, so a heat pump, using about a third as much electricity for the same heat, costs far less to run on the same tariff. How insulation changes the running cost

Better insulation lowers the heat a home needs, and lets the heat pump run cooler and more efficiently. For a 3 bed semi on a heat pump tariff: poor insulation £853 a year, average £705, good £546 and excellent £374. With good insulation a heat pump costs less to run than gas even on the standard rate: £726 against £745. The insulation calculator shows which measures your home still needs.

How we work it out

Heat needed. The median gas used by homes of your type, size and insulation, from meter readings for 39,502 gas heated homes in the government's National Energy Efficiency Data-Framework, multiplied by 90% boiler efficiency. It includes hot water. If you enter your own gas, oil, LPG or electricity use, we use that instead: oil and LPG boilers are taken as 85% efficient, and oil is converted at 10.294 kWh a litre.

Heat pump efficiency. The whole system efficiency measured in 428 air source heat pumps by the Electrification of Heat trial, which counts hot water, the backup heater and the pumps. The trial's median was 2.78, and the middle half of homes ran between 2.55 and 3.05. We use 2.55 for poor insulation, 2.78 for average and 3.05 for good, and 3.25, our estimate near the top of the trial's range, for excellent. A heat pump's own SCOP on a data sheet is usually higher.

Prices. Electricity and gas at the Ofgem price cap for October to December 2026, paying by direct debit. The heat pump tariff figure of 19.8p is our estimate for Cosy Octopus in October 2026, from its September rates, with 60% of heating in its cheap hours and 10% in the 4pm to 7pm peak, as costed in our tariff guide.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a heat pump cost to run a month?

For a typical 3 bed semi with average insulation, about £59 a month averaged over the year on a heat pump tariff at about 19.8p per kWh, or £78 a month on the standard price cap rate of 26.32p. Winter months cost several times more than summer ones, when the heat pump only makes hot water.

Is a heat pump cheaper to run than a gas boiler?

On a heat pump tariff, yes: about £705 a year for a 3 bed semi against £877 with a gas boiler, before standing charges. On the standard electricity rate it costs about £60 a year more. At the measured median efficiency a heat pump beats gas at any electricity price below about 24.6p per kWh.

Why is my installer's running cost lower than yours?

Installers usually quote the seasonal efficiency (SCOP) of the heat pump itself, often 3.5 to 4. We use 2.78, the median measured across 428 real homes in the government's Electrification of Heat trial for the whole system, including hot water, backup heating and pumps, which is what the meter records. A well designed system can beat it; choose your installer's figure in the calculator to see the difference.

Does a heat pump get rid of the gas standing charge?

Only if you have the gas supply capped, which most people do once the boiler is gone and nothing else uses gas. That saves the gas standing charge of 29.68p a day, £108.33 a year at the October to December 2026 price cap, on top of any running cost saving.
