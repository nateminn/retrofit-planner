Boiler Upgrade Scheme: How to Claim £7,500 Towards a Heat Pump

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme (BUS) provides a grant of £7,500 towards the cost of an air source or ground source heat pump, or £9,000 until 31 March 2027 if the home is heated by oil or LPG and has no mains gas connection. For a typical 3-bed semi, where the whole air source heat pump job costs £11,000 to £15,500 installed, the £7,500 grant brings it down to £3,500 to £8,000. The scheme runs to 2030 and is open to property owners in England and Wales replacing fossil fuel or electric heating, with no income test and no benefits test. This guide covers eligibility, how to apply, what the grant covers, and how to combine it with other funding for the lowest possible cost.

The grant is deducted directly from your installer's invoice. You never receive the money yourself and you never need to pay the full amount upfront. You pay only the balance after the grant is applied.

Who is eligible?

The BUS is available to property owners in England and Wales who meet the following criteria:

You must be replacing fossil fuel or electric heating. This includes gas, oil and LPG boilers, coal or other solid fuel, and electric heating such as storage heaters or panel radiators. You cannot get a grant to replace a system that is already low carbon, such as an existing heat pump, and a home with no heating system at all does not qualify.

You no longer need a valid EPC. Since the scheme's amended regulations came into force on 28 April 2026, the installer records the certificate number if the home has a valid EPC, or the details of an expired one, but a missing certificate no longer stops the application. You can look yours up on the GOV.UK EPC register.

The heat pump must be installed by an MCS-certified installer. MCS certification ensures the installer meets quality standards and the system is designed correctly for your property. Find certified installers through the MCS directory.

Most new builds are excluded. A new build that the developer is still building is not eligible, but if you move into a finished new build with a fossil fuel boiler you may be able to get the grant. A self build is eligible if you or the original owner built it, or paid a builder to, and it has never been owned by a business.

Landlords can apply, social housing cannot. The grant covers second homes and homes you rent out, as long as you own them. Social housing is excluded. If you own a rental property with fossil fuel or electric heating, you can claim the BUS grant to install a heat pump. Read our EPC guide for landlords for how a heat pump affects your rental obligations.

Insulate before applying

Better insulation means a smaller, cheaper heat pump. Loft insulation and cavity wall insulation should be done before the heat pump is installed. If you qualify for ECO4, get the free insulation first, then apply for BUS. This reduces your heat pump cost and running bills.

ECO4 closes on 31 December 2026. Work under the scheme has to be done before then, so if you think you qualify, start now. The government has confirmed there will be no successor supplier obligation; instead it has committed £1.5 billion of extra grant funding for households on low incomes. Check what you qualify for.

How to apply

Step 1: Insulate your home. Address loft insulation, wall insulation, and draught-proofing first. This is not a BUS requirement, but it is strongly recommended. Your MCS installer will assess heat loss and may recommend insulation before sizing the heat pump.

Step 2: Get MCS installer quotes. Use the MCS directory to find certified heat pump installers in your area. Get at least three quotes. Each should include a full heat loss calculation specific to your property.

Step 3: Choose your installer and agree the quote. The quote should clearly show the total cost, the BUS grant deduction, and the balance you pay. Check that the installer is applying for the correct grant amount.

Step 4: Your installer applies for the grant. The installer submits the application through the Ofgem BUS portal. Ofgem then contacts you to confirm the installer is acting for you, and you have to confirm your consent before the voucher is issued.

Step 5: Installation. Once the voucher is issued, the installer has three months to finish an air source heat pump, or six months for a ground source heat pump, or it has to apply again. If the system was fitted first, the application has to be made within 120 days of commissioning. The installer commissions the system, sets up controls, and shows you how to operate it.

Step 6: Payment. You pay the installer the balance after the grant deduction, and the installer claims the grant from Ofgem once the system is installed and checked. The installer must not ask you to pay the deducted amount. You do not need to handle any grant paperwork beyond the consent.

What does the grant cover?

The BUS grant is a fixed sum taken off the installer's quote for the whole job. The installed costs recorded under the scheme, which our figures use, cover the system itself, labour and VAT, as installers report them on the MCS certificate. Ask whether your quote includes the hot water cylinder and any radiator upgrades your home needs. Insulation is a separate cost.

Combining BUS with other grants

The BUS grant can be combined with other funding to reduce your total retrofit cost even further.

BUS plus ECO4: Use ECO4 to fund free insulation (loft, cavity wall, or solid wall), then BUS to fund the heat pump. ECO4 closes on 31 December 2026. In England, the council-run Warm Homes: Local Grant can also sit alongside the BUS, but not for the same measure.

BUS plus 0% VAT: Heat pump installations carry 0% VAT until 31 March 2027, after which they return to the 5% reduced rate. On an £11,000 to £15,500 job that is worth about £550 to £780. Your installer applies it, so there is nothing to claim.

BUS plus solar panels: Installing solar panels alongside a heat pump provides cheap daytime electricity to power the heat pump, further reducing running costs. Solar panels also qualify for 0% VAT.

Common questions and concerns

Will a heat pump heat my home adequately?

Yes, if correctly designed. An MCS installer performs a room-by-room heat loss calculation to size the system. With adequate insulation and appropriately sized radiators, a heat pump keeps your whole home at 21°C. See our guide for old houses for specific advice on period properties.

What about noise?

Modern air source heat pumps are quiet enough for most gardens, and permitted development rules set a noise limit at your neighbour's nearest window that the installer checks when choosing where the unit goes. Anti-vibration pads reduce transmitted vibration, and our heat pump noise guide covers the rest.

Do I need planning permission?

Most installations fall under permitted development. Listed buildings and conservation areas have restrictions. Read our planning permission guide for the full rules.

What happens if the scheme runs out of money?

The BUS has been extended and expanded. The Warm Homes Plan funds it with £2.7 billion from 2025/26 to 2029/30, regulations that came into force on 28 April 2026 extended it to 2030, and the budget for 2026 to 2027 is £400 million. Funding is set year by year, and the extra £1,500 for oil and LPG homes ends on 31 March 2027, so if you are ready there is no advantage in waiting.

Frequently asked questions

How much is the Boiler Upgrade Scheme grant?

£7,500 for an air source or ground source heat pump, rising to £9,000 until 31 March 2027 if the home is heated by oil or LPG and has no mains gas connection. Air-to-air heat pumps get £2,500 and biomass boilers in rural, off gas homes £5,000. It is taken off your installer's invoice, so for a typical 3-bed semi costing £11,000 to £15,500 installed you pay £3,500 to £8,000. Use our calculator to see your cost after the grant.

Who is eligible for the Boiler Upgrade Scheme?

Anyone who owns a property in England or Wales, including landlords and second home owners, and is replacing fossil fuel or electric heating with a heat pump fitted by an MCS-certified installer. There is no income or benefits test, and since 28 April 2026 you no longer need a valid EPC. Social housing and most new builds are excluded.

How do I apply for the BUS grant?

You do not apply yourself. Your MCS-certified installer applies through Ofgem once you have agreed a quote, and Ofgem contacts you to confirm your consent. Once the voucher is issued the installer has three months to finish an air source heat pump, or six months for ground source, and you pay only the balance after the grant.

When does the Boiler Upgrade Scheme end?

The scheme runs to 2030. The Warm Homes Plan funds it with £2.7 billion to 2029/30, and the budget for 2026 to 2027 is £400 million. The extra £1,500 for oil and LPG homes ends sooner, on 31 March 2027. Read our heat pump guide for old houses to prepare your property first.

Can I combine BUS with ECO4 or other grants?

Yes. Use ECO4 for free insulation until 31 December 2026 and BUS for the heat pump. You also pay 0% VAT on the installation until 31 March 2027. In England the Warm Homes: Local Grant can fund other measures in the same home, but not the same measure twice. Check our grant eligibility tool for the full picture.
