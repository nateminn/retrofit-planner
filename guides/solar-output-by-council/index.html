Solar panel output by council

The short answer

  • 981 kWh a year for each kW of panels on a south facing roof in the median UK council, about 3,924 kWh from a 4 kW system
  • Eastbourne makes the most, 1,136 kWh; the Shetland Islands the least, 752 kWh
  • Your roof matters more: half of all councils are within 6% of the median, while facing east or west costs about 20%

In the median UK council, each kW of solar panels on a south facing roof makes 981 kWh a year, about 3,924 kWh from a typical 4 kW system. That runs from 1,136 kWh in Eastbourne to 752 kWh in the Shetland Islands.

How much does location matter?

Less than your roof. Half of all councils are within 6% of the median; turning panels from south to east or west costs about 20%.

Shading, roof direction and pitch usually matter more than which council you live in. The solar calculator takes your postcode, roof direction and pitch, and uses the PVGIS figure for your postcode district less 7% for shading and dirt on the panels.

Frequently asked questions

Which part of the UK gets the most solar power?

The south coast of England. Eastbourne tops the table at 1,136 kWh a year for each kW of panels on a south facing roof, followed by Hastings and Brighton and Hove.

Do solar panels work in Scotland?

Yes. A south facing roof in the average Scottish council makes 850 kWh a year for each kW of panels, about 13% less than the UK median of 981 kWh. Payback takes longer; see solar payback in Scotland.

How are these figures worked out?

We ran the European Commission's PVGIS model for up to 8 places where people live in each council, and averaged them: 1 kW of panels facing due south at a 35 degree pitch with 14% losses, on the SARAH3 sunshine record for 2005 to 2023.
