Is Loft Insulation Worth It? Costs, Savings, and Payback for Every UK Home

Usually, yes, but for different reasons than most sites give. Loft insulation is cheap, lasts about 40 years and is often free through a grant. What it takes off your bill is smaller than many calculators claim. When the government compared gas use before and after in 1,486 homes against similar homes that had nothing done, the median home used 3.2% less gas after loft insulation. For a 3 bed semi heated by gas that is about £28 a year at October 2026 prices. The Energy Saving Trust puts the cost at £750 to lay 270mm in a bare loft and £600 to top up a thin layer, so at full price a £600 top-up takes about 21 years to pay back in a 3 bed semi. A loft with little or nothing in it usually saves more than the median, and part of the gain shows up as a warmer home rather than a lower bill. This guide gives the measured figures for your type of home so you can decide on real numbers.

Loft insulation costs far less than wall insulation, and at full price it pays back much sooner than solid wall insulation, which on measured savings takes more than 50 years. It is the right first step because it is cheap, quick and low risk, not because it transforms your bill.

What if you already have some insulation?

Many UK lofts already have some insulation, often well short of the 270mm recommended today. If yours is below 270mm, topping up is usually worth doing, especially if a grant pays for it.

Long-term value: the 40-year picture

The Energy Saving Trust gives loft insulation a lifespan of around 40 years if it is installed correctly and kept dry. Small yearly savings add up over that time.

For a 3 bed semi saving about £28 a year, 40 years of savings come to about £1,100 at today's prices, or about £2,100 if energy prices rise by 3% a year. Against a £600 top-up, that is roughly two to three and a half times the cost. For a 3 bed mid-terrace saving about £25 a year, the 40 year total is about £1,000 at today's prices.

These numbers leave out the other gains: a higher EPC score and a warmer home. Measured savings are lower than modelled ones partly because many homes were under-heated before, and take some of the gain as warmth rather than a lower bill. That warmth is a real benefit; it just does not show on the bill.

When loft insulation is not worth it

Loft insulation is worth it for almost every UK home, but there are a few situations where it makes less sense.

You already have 270mm or more. Once you reach 270mm, adding more delivers negligible additional savings. Check your current depth before buying materials. See our loft insulation lifespan guide for how to inspect what you have.

Your loft is converted into a living space. If your loft is a bedroom or office, you need insulation between the rafters rather than on the floor. The Energy Saving Trust says this is a job for a trained installer and costs much more than insulating the loft floor, so the payback is longer.

You have damp or structural issues. Insulation makes the loft colder, which makes condensation more likely and can worsen existing damp. If your roof leaks or the loft is damp, get professional advice and fix the cause before insulating, as the Energy Saving Trust advises. Wet insulation does little.

Your loft contains asbestos. The Health and Safety Executive says any home built or refurbished before 2000 may contain asbestos, for example in loose fill insulation or an old cement water tank. If you suspect asbestos, do not disturb it. Get it tested first. Our insulation lifespan guide covers how to identify potential asbestos risk.

Free loft insulation: who qualifies?

If you receive a qualifying benefit, the ECO4 scheme can fund professional loft insulation at no cost until it closes on 31 December 2026. Qualifying benefits include Universal Credit, Pension Credit, Child Benefit, Housing Benefit, Income Support, and income-based JSA or income-related ESA. A home you own must have an EPC rating of D to G, and a privately rented home E to G.

Even if you do not receive benefits, your local council may refer you through ECO4 Flex if you are on a low income, or, in England, fund the work through a Warm Homes: Local Grant. Use our grant eligibility checker to see what is available.

If you qualify for free insulation, there is no reason to do it yourself. ECO4 covers professional installation, which is quicker, comes with a warranty, and is done to PAS 2035 quality standards.

Check for free insulation before paying

Even if you think you will not qualify, it is worth checking. ECO4 Flex eligibility varies by council. Five minutes checking could save you the £600 to £750 cost. Use our grant eligibility checker.

Loft insulation and your EPC rating

Loft insulation raises your EPC score, and for a home on the borderline between bands it can be enough to move up a band. How much it adds depends on what the loft has now.

This matters most for landlords, who must meet minimum EPC standards to let a property. Where the loft is under-insulated, it is often among the first recommendations on an EPC report and one of the cheapest ways to gain points. It is also relevant if you are selling, because buyers see your EPC.

Loft insulation before a heat pump

If you are considering a heat pump for your old house, insulating the loft first is essential. Better insulation reduces the heat demand, which means a smaller, cheaper heat pump with lower running costs.

Insulation helps twice: once on your heating bills, and again by reducing the size of any future heat pump. In our model a 3 bed semi with poor insulation needs about a 10 kW heat pump and one with good insulation about 7.5 kW. At the median price paid under the Boiler Upgrade Scheme that is about £13,300 against £12,300 installed, before the grant. Loft insulation alone will not make that whole difference, but it is the cheapest part of it, along with cavity wall insulation and draught-proofing.

How to get started

Option 1: DIY. Read our step-by-step DIY guide. It covers materials, safety equipment, installation instructions, and common mistakes. You need mineral wool rolls, a dust mask, gloves, and a few hours.

Option 2: Professional installation. The Energy Saving Trust puts it at £750 for a bare loft and £600 for a top-up. It usually takes a few hours, with little disruption to your living space. Use a TrustMark registered installer and get more than one quote.

Option 3: Free through a grant. If you qualify, you can have it professionally installed at no cost through ECO4, which closes on 31 December 2026, or a council Warm Homes: Local Grant. Check eligibility with our grant checker or read the full ECO4 guide.

Whichever route you choose, loft insulation is cheap, low risk and long lasting. Just expect a modest saving on the bill and a warmer home, not a transformed one.

Frequently asked questions

Is loft insulation worth the money?

Usually, yes. It costs £600 to £750 according to the Energy Saving Trust, lasts about 40 years and is often free through a grant. The saving on your bill is modest: homes the government measured used a median 3.2% less gas afterwards, about £28 a year for a 3 bed semi on gas, so a £600 top-up takes about 21 years to pay back at full price. A loft with little or nothing in it usually saves more.

How much does loft insulation save per year?

Homes the government measured used a median 3.2% less gas after loft insulation. On gas at 7.97p per kWh that is about £18 a year for a 2 bed flat, £25 for a 3 bed mid-terrace, £28 for a 3 bed semi, £30 for a 3 bed detached house and £37 for a 4 bed detached house. Homes heated by oil, LPG or electricity save more in pounds.

How long does it take for loft insulation to pay for itself?

At full price, about 20 to 25 years for a typical house. A £600 top-up takes about 21 years in a 3 bed semi on gas, and £750 for a bare loft takes about 27 years at the median saving, although a bare loft usually saves more than the median. If a grant pays for it, you save from the first winter.

Should I top up existing loft insulation?

If you have less than 270mm, usually yes, and especially if a grant pays for it. The Energy Saving Trust puts a top-up from 120mm to 270mm at about £600. The thinner your current layer, the more you gain. If you already have close to 270mm, there is little left to gain.

Can I get free loft insulation?

Yes, if you qualify. ECO4 funds loft insulation for many households on means-tested benefits until 31 December 2026, and councils run Warm Homes: Local Grants for lower income households. The Great British Insulation Scheme closed on 31 March 2026.
