Best Electricity Tariffs for Heat Pump Owners in the UK

Nine electricity tariffs built for heat pump homes are open to new customers in September 2026, and the best of them cuts the heating bill of a 3-bed semi by about £280 a year against the standard variable rate, or about £420 for a 4-bed detached house. The tariff you choose is the single biggest factor in whether a heat pump costs more or less to run than gas. This guide compares every open tariff on its real rates and windows, works out what each one saves, and says which to pick.

Heat pump tariffs open in September 2026

Every tariff below needs a smart meter that sends half-hourly readings, and none of them lets the supplier control your heating. Rates are for a typical region and change with the price cap or the fixed period, so check your own postcode before you switch.

Our recommendation

For most heat pump owners, Cosy Octopus is still the tariff to beat. Eight cheap hours a day in three windows, no tie to any heat pump brand, no exit fee, and a 1pm to 4pm window that lets you heat the cylinder and warm the house on the mildest part of the day, when the heat pump is at its most efficient. The catch is the 4pm to 7pm peak, about 50 percent above the day rate. A home that cannot coast through those three hours on stored warmth loses part of the saving.

If you would rather have no peak penalty at all, EDF Heat Pump Tracker is the simplest option on the market: two three-hour windows at 10p below your regional cap rate, the cap rate the rest of the time, no exit fee and no change to the standing charge. It saves less than Cosy on paper but it cannot punish you.

E.ON Next Pumped suits homes that heat mostly overnight and have a large hot water cylinder, because its cheapest band runs from 10pm to 6am. Intelligent Octopus Go has the lowest off-peak rate of all at 8p, but you need a compatible electric car to join, so it is an EV tariff that happens to be good for a heat pump. Octopus Agile can beat everything when a smart controller shifts the heating around the price, and can cost more than the standard rate when nothing does.

You need a smart meter

Every tariff on this page needs a smart meter that sends half-hourly readings, which means a SMETS2 meter or an older SMETS1 meter that has been enrolled. Your supplier fits one free, the visit takes about an hour, and you should book it before you apply, because the tariff cannot go live without it.

What changed in 2026

OVO Heat Pump Plus closed. OVO stopped taking new customers in December 2025 and withdrew the tariff on 1 February 2026. Existing customers lost the 15p heat pump rate and moved to OVO's standard variable tariff. There is no successor, so OVO customers with a heat pump now need to switch supplier to get a time-of-use rate.

Cosy Octopus grew to eight cheap hours. The three windows now total eight hours a day, up from six, and a 12-month fixed version launched in June 2026 alongside the variable one. The fixed rates were set during the summer wholesale spike, so on 14 September 2026 the fix costs more per unit than the variable tariff. Compare both on the day you switch.

New entrants. EDF, E.ON Next, British Gas, ScottishPower and Good Energy all now run a dedicated heat pump tariff, so Octopus is no longer the only choice. Every one of them requires a smart meter and none requires a particular heat pump brand.

VAT on electricity is zero from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027. Gas keeps its 5 percent rate. That is why the October cap rate of 26.32p is only 0.8 percent above the summer rate, and why you should compare tariffs on their October prices rather than on figures published in the summer.

How to shift heating to off-peak

The saving from any of these tariffs depends on how much of your heating you can move into the cheap hours. These are the habits that make the difference.

Warm the house before the window closes. On Cosy Octopus and EDF Heat Pump Tracker, run the heating from 4am so the house is up to temperature by 7am. A well-insulated home then holds most of that warmth through the morning. The better your loft insulation and draught-proofing, the longer it lasts.

Heat the cylinder in the afternoon. The 1pm to 4pm window on Cosy, EDF, British Gas and Good Energy is the warmest part of the day, when the heat pump gets the most heat from each unit of electricity. Set the hot water to heat then, and set the cylinder to 50 degrees so a 200 to 250 litre tank carries the household through to the next window.

Coast through the peak. On tariffs with a 4pm to 7pm peak, set the thermostat one or two degrees lower for those three hours. In a home with decent insulation the drop is barely noticeable, and it avoids paying 40 to 50p a unit.

Let a controller do it. A smart thermostat that knows your tariff windows schedules all of this for you, and on Agile it is the only sensible way to run a heat pump.

Use thermal mass. Solid floors, brick walls, big radiators and a large cylinder all store heat. Homes with plenty of it can heat hard in the cheap hours and coast through the expensive ones. Homes with little of it should favour EDF's tariff, which has no peak to avoid.

Standing charges and exit fees

Unit rates get the attention, but the daily standing charge is paid every day of the year regardless. Cosy Octopus sits at about 42p a day in London and 51p in the East Midlands. EDF and British Gas keep their standard charges, 43p to 68p depending on region. Good Energy charges the most, up to 74p a day in North Wales and Merseyside, which is about £60 a year more than Octopus in the same area, and it is the only tariff here with an exit fee, £75. Cosy Octopus, EDF Heat Pump Tracker, E.ON Next Pumped and British Gas Heat Power all have no exit fee.

Switching tariff

Switching is free and takes 1 to 5 working days once the smart meter is sending readings. EDF asks you to join its standard tariff first and then move to Heat Pump Tracker, and ScottishPower does the same for new customers. British Gas only takes credit customers paying by Direct Debit. Octopus and E.ON let you apply directly. If you are part way through a fixed deal with another supplier, check the exit fee; moving to the same supplier's heat pump tariff is usually free.

Combining a heat pump tariff with solar panels

With solar panels, the heat pump runs on free electricity through spring and summer days, and the tariff supplies cheap power for the winter. The 1pm to 4pm windows on Cosy and EDF line up with the solar peak, so on a bright winter afternoon the cylinder heats almost for nothing. Add a battery and you can charge it in the cheap window as well, then run the evening peak from stored power. A well-insulated 3-bed semi with solar, a battery and Cosy Octopus can bring annual heating costs below £500. Frequently asked questions

What is the best electricity tariff for a heat pump?

Cosy Octopus for most homes: eight cheap hours a day, no brand tie-in, no exit fee, and the biggest modelled saving at about £280 a year for a 3-bed semi. EDF Heat Pump Tracker is the best choice if you want cheap windows with no evening peak penalty.

How much can I save with a heat pump tariff?

Between 12 and 26 percent of your heating cost, depending on the tariff and how much heating you can shift into the cheap hours. For a 3-bed semi that is £130 to £280 a year, and for a 4-bed detached house about £420 on Cosy Octopus. Use the heat pump calculator for your own property.

Do I need a smart meter for a heat pump tariff?

Yes. Every tariff on this page needs a smart meter sending half-hourly readings. Your supplier fits one free, and the tariff cannot start until it is working.

Do I need an Octopus heat pump to get Cosy Octopus?

No. Cosy Octopus is open to any air source or ground source heat pump, electric boiler or electric radiator system, whoever installed it. Octopus's own Cosy heat pumps are designed around the tariff but there is no tie-in either way.

Can I use Octopus Agile with a heat pump?

Yes, but only with controls that move the heating away from the expensive half hours. With automation Agile matched Cosy in our model; without it, September 2026 prices made it cost more than the standard rate.

Is Economy 7 still worth it for a heat pump?

Rarely. Its night rate of 14 to 18p is higher than every dedicated heat pump tariff and its day rate is steeper, so it saves about £85 a year on a 3-bed semi against £200 to £280 for the tariffs above. It only makes sense where a smart meter cannot be fitted.
