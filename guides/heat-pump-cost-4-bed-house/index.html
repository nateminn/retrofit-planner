Heat Pump Cost for a 4-Bedroom House: Full 2026 Breakdown

The short answer

  • £12,500 to £18,000 installed, or £5,000 to £10,500 after the £7,500 grant (£3,500 to £9,000 for oil or LPG homes)
  • About 12 kW is the typical size for a 4-bed detached
  • £936 a year to run on a heat pump tariff, £228 less than gas
Real pricesCost breakdownSizeRunning costsIs it worth it?QuotesFAQs

An air source heat pump for a 4-bed house costs £12,500 to £18,000 installed. That is the middle half of what installers recorded for a heat pump of the size a 4-bed needs under the Boiler Upgrade Scheme, with a median of £14,700 for a 4-bed detached, and it is the full installed cost the installer recorded, labour and VAT included. The £7,500 Boiler Upgrade Scheme grant brings that down to £5,000 to £10,500. Homes heated by oil or LPG with no mains gas connection get £9,000 until 31 March 2027, which leaves £3,500 to £9,000. At the Ofgem price cap for October to December 2026, running costs are £936 a year on a heat pump tariff, £228 less than gas. This guide covers every cost that is specific to a 4-bed home: the prices people actually paid, sizing, the models installers fit, radiators and cylinders, running costs, and what to check in a quote.

Not every 4-bed house is detached. A 4-bed semi shares one wall, so you might expect it to need less heat. In the measured consumption data it does not: a 4-bed semi uses slightly more heat a year than a 4-bed detached, 13,770 kWh against 13,140 kWh at average insulation, because a house with four bedrooms and only one shared wall is usually a large semi. It therefore needs a heat pump of the same size or slightly bigger, and costs about the same to install. Do not budget on the semi being cheaper.

Your MCS installer must carry out a room-by-room heat loss calculation before quoting. It works out how much heat each room loses on a cold design day and sizes the unit to cover the total and your hot water with a small margin. Our model's sizes are a guide to the band you will be quoted in, not a substitute for that survey. Oversizing is not a safe default. A unit that is too big costs more to buy and switches on and off more often in mild weather, so treat a quote with no heat loss calculation behind it as a warning sign.

Insulate first to shrink the heat pump

Loft insulation and cavity wall insulation reduce the size of heat pump you need and the running cost for good. The Energy Saving Trust puts a loft top-up from 120mm to 270mm at about £600 and cavity wall insulation for a typical home at about £2,700. On our model, moving a 4-bed detached from poorly to well insulated takes the heat pump from 13.5 kW to 10 kW, about £2,100 off the median installed price, and £407 a year off the running cost on a heat pump tariff. Measured savings from individual measures are smaller than that full step: in the government's NEED data, homes that had cavity wall and loft insulation fitted together used a median 8.4% less gas. If you qualify for ECO4, get the insulation done first, because the scheme only pays for work finished by 31 December 2026.

Radiators, cylinder and pipework

Expect 4 to 7 of the 10 to 14 radiators to need upsizing, plus a heat pump cylinder. The cylinder is in the recorded job cost; microbore pipework is the common budget breaker on long runs.

Read the detail

A heat pump runs the heating at a lower water temperature than a gas boiler, so each radiator has to be larger to deliver the same heat. A 4-bed house typically has 10 to 14 radiators, and 4 to 7 of them need upsizing, usually the living room, the hall and landing and the largest bedrooms. Our radiator sizing guide explains how installers decide which ones to change.

You also need a hot water cylinder, and the recorded job costs above include one. A family of four or five needs a larger cylinder than a couple, and the installer sizes it from your household's hot water use. If you currently have a combi boiler there is no cylinder, so you need a cupboard or utility space for one; the Energy Saving Trust says a cylinder can usually fit in any cupboard at least 80cm by 80cm. If you already have a system boiler with a cylinder, it will usually need replacing anyway, because heat pump cylinders have a larger coil to work at lower temperatures.

The common budget breaker is microbore pipework, the 8mm or 10mm pipes fitted in many homes from the 1970s onwards. It restricts flow at heat pump temperatures and often needs replacing with 15mm or 22mm pipe, and on a big house the runs are long. Ask the installer to check the pipe sizes during the survey rather than discovering the problem on installation day.

Running costs: 4-bed detached

A heat pump costs £936 a year on a heat pump tariff, £228 less than gas, but £1,244 on standard electricity. That is £80 a year more than gas, so the tariff decides the economics.

These figures use the Ofgem price cap for 1 October to 31 December 2026: electricity 26.32p per kWh and gas 7.97p per kWh. Gas rose about 9% at this cap change. Electricity barely moved because the government removed VAT from electricity bills from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027, so when 5% VAT returns every heat pump figure below rises by about 5%.

The tariff makes or breaks the economics. On a heat pump tariff you save £228 a year against gas at today's prices, or about £181 once VAT returns to electricity. On a standard tariff the heat pump costs £80 a year more than gas, or about £142 with VAT. Disconnecting your gas supply saves a further £108 a year in gas standing charge. Read our running costs guide for the full comparison, our tariff guide for how to get an effective rate near 19.8p, and our electric boiler vs heat pump guide if you heat with electricity today.

Insulation moves these numbers as much as the tariff does. A well insulated 4-bed detached with 11,169 kWh of heat demand costs £989 a year on gas, £964 on a heat pump with a standard tariff and £725 on a heat pump tariff. Better insulation also lifts the seasonal efficiency, so at that level the heat pump edges ahead of gas even on a standard tariff, and the saving on a heat pump tariff rises to £264 a year. Against oil, the heat pump saves £811 a year on a heat pump tariff and £503 on standard electricity, with heating oil at 11.3p per kWh, the BoilerJuice UK average of 116.20p a litre on 25 September 2026. Oil prices move, so the £1,747 oil bill in the table is today's figure rather than a fixed one.

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme for a 4-bed

A 4-bed gets the same £7,500 grant, or £9,000 for oil or LPG homes off the gas grid until 31 March 2027. There is no limit on house value, and the installer deducts it from the invoice.

Read the detail

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme pays £7,500 towards an air source or ground source heat pump in England and Wales. Until 31 March 2027, a home heated by oil or LPG that has no mains gas connection gets an extra £1,500, making £9,000. Your MCS certified installer applies for the grant and it comes off the invoice, so you never handle the money. Regulations in force from 28 April 2026 extended the scheme to 2030 and removed the need for a valid EPC, although your installer records one if the home has it, and installations carry 0% VAT until 31 March 2027. There is no limit on the value of the house, and the 45 kW cap on a single system is far above anything a 4-bed needs, so a 4-bed detached qualifies exactly as a flat does, provided you own it and the heat pump replaces fossil fuel heating such as gas, oil, LPG or electric heating. Hybrid systems that keep a boiler do not qualify. Read our BUS grant guide for the full eligibility rules and our grant checker for everything else you might qualify for.

Is a heat pump worth it for a 4-bed house

On mains gas it is a weak case. At £228 a year on a heat pump tariff, the extra over a new boiler takes about 5 to 29 years to earn back. Against oil it saves £811 a year.

Read the detail

The government puts the average cost of a new gas boiler at about £3,500 including VAT, and our boiler vs heat pump comparison scales that by home size to about £3,800 for four bedrooms. Against that, a typical 4-bed detached heat pump job costs £1,200 to £6,700 more after the £7,500 grant, plus any radiator work that is not in the quote. At £228 a year saving on a heat pump tariff, that is a payback of about 5 to 29 years, or 7 to 37 years once VAT returns to electricity, and on a standard electricity tariff there is no saving to pay anything back at all. On mains gas, then, a 4-bed detached is a weak case on running cost alone: at the median price the extra takes about 15 years to earn back even on a heat pump tariff, and it pays back quickly only if you land near the low end of the cost range. Off the gas grid the case is much stronger. Against oil the saving is £811 a year with oil at 11.3p per kWh, the BoilerJuice UK average on 25 September 2026, although oil prices move, and the £9,000 grant takes the whole job to £3,500 to £9,000 if you claim before 31 March 2027, with no oil tank to maintain afterwards. Getting quotes: what to check

Get three MCS quotes and compare the design, not the headline price. Each should include a heat loss calculation, a radiator schedule, a sound calculation and the £7,500 or £9,000 grant deducted from the invoice.

Read the detail

Get three quotes from MCS certified installers, and compare them on the design rather than the headline price. Each quote should include:

  • A room-by-room heat loss calculation with the design outdoor temperature stated.
  • The design flow temperature, ideally 50°C or lower, and the seasonal performance the installer expects at that temperature.
  • A radiator schedule listing every emitter, which ones change, their new sizes, and whether that work is in the price.
  • The cylinder size and where it will go.
  • An MCS 020(a) sound calculation for the outdoor unit position, showing it meets the 37 dB limit at the nearest neighbour's window.
  • The warranty on the unit and on the installation, and who you call when something fails.
  • Confirmation that the installer will apply for the Boiler Upgrade Scheme at the £7,500 or £9,000 rate and deduct it from the invoice.

Installation usually takes several days for the heat pump and cylinder, and longer if most radiators are being swapped. Check the planning permission rules before you sign. Most 4-bed houses in England are covered by permitted development, except listed buildings and, in conservation areas, units on a wall or roof facing a road. Find installers through the MCS installer search, or use the quote form on this page to ask up to three MCS certified installers covering your postcode to contact you.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a heat pump cost for a 4-bedroom house?

£12,500 to £18,000 installed, the middle half of what installers recorded under the Boiler Upgrade Scheme for a heat pump of the size a 4-bed needs, including the cylinder, labour and VAT. After the £7,500 BUS grant a 4-bed detached costs £5,000 to £10,500, or £3,500 to £9,000 with the £9,000 grant for oil and LPG homes with no mains gas. Radiator upgrades can add to that where they are needed.

What size heat pump does a 4-bed house need?

About 12 kW for a typical 4-bed detached house and 12.5 kW for a 4-bed semi on our model, or around 10 kW if the house is well insulated. The installer's heat loss calculation decides. Insulate first to bring the size and cost down.

How much does it cost to run a heat pump in a 4-bed house?

£936 a year on a heat pump tariff and £1,244 on standard electricity, against £1,164 for gas, at the October to December 2026 price cap for a 4-bed detached with 13,140 kWh of heat demand. Electricity carries no VAT until 31 March 2027, so the heat pump figures rise by about 5% after that. See our running costs guide.

Which heat pump is best for a 4-bed detached house?

Any correctly sized unit of around 10 to 14 kW from Vaillant, Mitsubishi, Daikin or Samsung can heat a 4-bed detached house well. Choose on the installer's design, a flow temperature of 50°C or lower and the seasonal performance in the quote, not on brand. Our heat pump noise guide covers the outdoor unit.

Is a heat pump worth it for a 4-bedroom house?

Off the gas grid, usually. On mains gas it depends on the tariff. A 4-bed detached saves £228 a year against gas on a heat pump tariff and about £811 against oil at 11.3p per kWh, the BoilerJuice UK average on 25 September 2026. On a standard electricity tariff it costs about £80 a year more to run than a gas boiler, so there is no saving to bank. Compare with our boiler vs heat pump tool.

How long does a 4-bed heat pump installation take?

Usually several days for the heat pump, cylinder and controls, and longer when most radiators are replaced. The BUS grant is claimed by the installer and taken off the invoice, so there is no waiting on your side.
