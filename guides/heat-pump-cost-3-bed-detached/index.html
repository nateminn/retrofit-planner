Heat Pump Cost for a 3-Bed Detached House: Full 2026 Breakdown

The short answer

  • £11,000 to £15,500 installed, or £3,500 to £8,000 after the £7,500 grant (£2,000 to £6,500 for oil or LPG homes)
  • About 9.5 kW is the typical size for a 3-bed detached house
  • £750 a year to run on a heat pump tariff, £182 less than gas and £650 less than oil

An air source heat pump for a typical 3-bed detached house costs £11,000 to £15,500 installed. That is the middle half of what installers recorded for a heat pump of its size under the Boiler Upgrade Scheme, with a median of £13,000, and it is the full installed cost the installer recorded, labour and VAT included. The £7,500 Boiler Upgrade Scheme grant brings that down to £3,500 to £8,000, or £2,000 to £6,500 with the £9,000 grant for homes heated by oil or LPG with no mains gas connection, available until 31 March 2027. At the Ofgem price cap for October to December 2026 the running cost is £750 a year on a heat pump tariff, £182 less than gas and £650 less than oil at 11.3p per kWh, the BoilerJuice UK average on 25 September 2026. On a standard electricity tariff it is £997, about £65 a year more than gas. Four exposed walls put a detached house a little above a semi on heat demand, and this guide covers what that means for the unit, the radiators, the cylinder and the bill.

The best price evidence is what installers actually charged. Every heat pump paid for through the Boiler Upgrade Scheme in England and Wales has its installed cost recorded on its MCS certificate, and the government publishes the median by heat pump size. In 2025/26 the median air source installation cost £12,686 at 8 to 10 kW and £13,943 at 10 to 12 kW, including the system, labour and VAT, before the grant. Across all sizes, the middle half of jobs in April to June 2026 cost £11,128 to £15,627.

The government does not publish costs by house type, so we size the home first. Our heat model puts a typical 3-bed detached house at 10,530 kWh of heat a year, which needs a heat pump of about 9.5 kW, and reads the scheme's median for that size. It is still a compact job: 10 to 12 radiators and a cylinder that usually fits an existing airing cupboard or utility room.

What size heat pump for a 3-bed detached house

Around 8 to 10.5 kW, typically 9.5 kW. Insulating a typical house to well insulated drops it to 8 kW and takes about £600 off the median installed cost.

Read the detail

Most 3-bed detached houses need a heat pump of around 8 to 10.5 kW. Our model puts a typical one at 9.5 kW, a well insulated one at 8 kW and a poorly insulated one at 10.5 kW. For comparison, the median air source heat pump fitted under the scheme in April to June 2026 was 8 kW, and the middle half were 6 to 10.8 kW.

Heat loss scales with exposed surface, not floor area. A 3-bed semi and a 3-bed detached house can be the same size inside, but the semi shares one wall with a heated neighbour and loses little through it. The detached house loses heat through all four walls and has more external corners. In the measured data the difference is modest, which is why the two usually need heat pumps in the same size band.

The number that matters is the room-by-room heat loss calculation, not the bedroom count. A quote without one was sized by guesswork. An oversized unit costs more to buy and switches on and off more often in mild weather, and a 14 kW unit in a house that needs 9.5 kW is a mistake worth watching for in detached quotes.

Insulate first to shrink the heat pump

Loft insulation and cavity wall insulation are the cheap measures. The Energy Saving Trust puts a loft top-up from 120mm to 270mm at about £600 and cavity wall insulation for a typical home at about £2,200. On our model, taking a typical detached house to well insulated drops the heat pump from 9.5 kW to 8 kW, about £600 off the median installed cost, and from a poorly insulated start it is about £1,200. Measured in real homes, individual measures save less than that full step: in the government's NEED data, homes that had cavity wall and loft insulation fitted together used a median 8.4% less gas. Check your walls and loft with our insulation calculator, and if you receive a qualifying benefit ECO4 may pay for the insulation, but only for work finished by 31 December 2026.

Radiators, cylinder and pipework

Expect 4 to 6 of the 10 to 12 radiators to be upsized, plus a new cylinder. Microbore pipework, common from the 1970s, is the usual budget breaker and often needs replacing with 15mm or 22mm pipe.

Read the detail

A heat pump runs the heating at a lower water temperature than a boiler, so each radiator has to be larger to deliver the same warmth. A 3-bed detached house typically has 10 to 12 radiators, and 4 to 6 need upsizing, usually the living room, the hall and the bedrooms with two external walls. Our radiator sizing guide explains how installers decide which ones change.

You also need a hot water cylinder, and the recorded job costs above include one. If you have a combi boiler there is no cylinder today, so the installer needs somewhere to put it; the Energy Saving Trust says a cylinder can usually fit in any cupboard at least 80cm by 80cm, and most detached houses have an airing cupboard, utility room or attached garage that will take one. An existing cylinder usually still needs replacing, because heat pump cylinders have a larger coil.

The common budget breaker is microbore pipework, the 8mm or 10mm pipes fitted in many houses from the 1970s onwards. It restricts flow at heat pump temperatures and often needs replacing with 15mm or 22mm pipe. If the ground floor is an uninsulated suspended timber floor, common at this age, our underfloor insulation guide shows how to cut the heat loss through it.

Running costs: 3-bed detached on gas and oil

£750 a year on a heat pump tariff, £182 less than gas and £650 less than oil. On a standard tariff it costs £997, £65 more than gas, but still beats oil by £403.

Read the detail

The tariff makes or breaks the economics on gas. On a heat pump tariff you save £182 a year at today's prices, or about £144 once VAT returns to electricity, and disconnecting the gas supply saves a further £108 a year in standing charge. On a standard tariff you are £65 a year worse off, or about £115 with VAT, so on gas there is no running cost case until you move to a heat pump tariff. Read our running costs guide for the full comparison and our tariff guide for how to get an effective rate near 19.8p.

Oil changes the picture. Many 3-bed detached houses in villages run on oil, and at 11.3p per kWh, the BoilerJuice UK average of 116.20p a litre on 25 September 2026, through an 85% efficient boiler, 10,530 kWh of heat costs £1,400 a year. The heat pump beats that on either tariff: by £403 a year on standard electricity and by £650 on a heat pump tariff. Oil prices move, so treat those savings as today's figures rather than fixed ones. You also lose the tank and the price swings, and a detached plot usually has room for the outdoor unit, so the switch from oil is one of the more straightforward heat pump jobs.

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme: £7,500 or £9,000

£7,500, or £9,000 for oil or LPG homes with no mains gas until 31 March 2027. That leaves £3,500 to £8,000 to pay on gas, or £2,000 to £6,500 on oil or LPG.

Read the detail

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme pays £7,500 towards an air source or ground source heat pump in England and Wales. Until 31 March 2027, a home heated by oil or LPG that has no mains gas connection gets an extra £1,500, making £9,000. For a typical 3-bed detached house on gas that means paying £3,500 to £8,000 for the whole job; on oil or LPG with no mains gas it is £2,000 to £6,500. Your MCS certified installer applies for the grant and it comes off the invoice. Regulations in force from 28 April 2026 extended the scheme to 2030 and removed the need for a valid EPC, installations carry 0% VAT until 31 March 2027, and you need to own the home and be replacing fossil fuel heating. Leave the old oil boiler in place until the installer has surveyed it, because the higher rate depends on what the heat pump replaces.

Read our BUS grant guide for the eligibility rules and our grant checker for anything else you might qualify for. If your household income is low and the house is in England with an EPC of D to G, the council run Warm Homes: Local Grant can pay for insulation and a heat pump. The earlier Home Upgrade Grant is closed to applications, and our Warm Homes Plan guide explains how the new scheme works.

Is a heat pump worth it for a 3-bed detached house

On gas, only on a heat pump tariff. At £182 a year the payback runs from immediately to about 25 years. Against oil the case is clear, saving £650 a year.

Read the detail

The government puts the average cost of a new gas boiler at about £3,500 including VAT. Against that, a typical 3-bed detached heat pump job costs nothing to £4,500 more after the £7,500 grant, plus any radiator work that is not in the quote. At £182 a year saving on a heat pump tariff the payback runs from immediate to about 25 years, or up to about 31 years once VAT returns to electricity, and on a standard tariff there is no payback at all, because the heat pump costs £65 a year more to run than the boiler. On mains gas, then, a 3-bed detached house is not buying a heat pump mainly to save money. The case rests on a boiler that is due for replacement anyway, radiators that need little work, a heat pump tariff and the carbon saving.

Against oil the answer is simpler. With the £9,000 grant the whole heat pump job costs £2,000 to £6,500, and the heat pump saves £650 a year on a heat pump tariff with oil at 11.3p per kWh, so every £1,000 of extra cost over a new oil boiler pays back in about a year and a half, for as long as oil stays near that price. Claim before 31 March 2027 to get the higher grant. Run the numbers for your own house with our boiler vs heat pump comparison, and read heat pump vs new boiler for the 15-year view.

Ground source: realistic on a detached plot

Ground source is the one option a detached house unlocks. It needs a borehole or a large area of garden for a horizontal loop, and most semis cannot offer either while many detached gardens can. Under the scheme, the middle half of ground source installations in April to June 2026 cost £18,136 to £42,110, with a median of £27,232, and they get the same £7,500 grant. In return you get a system that usually runs more efficiently than air source, with no outdoor unit and no noise calculation. The government's heat pump trial measured too few ground source systems to give a reliable efficiency figure, so we do not price the running cost difference.

The upfront gap is large: roughly double the typical air source job. Ground source makes sense mainly when you are already digging, for an extension or a full landscaping job, or when the outdoor unit has nowhere acceptable to go. For most 3-bed detached houses air source is the right answer, and spare capital may be better spent finding out whether solar panels are worth it on a south-facing roof.

Placement, noise and planning on a detached plot

Placement is where a detached house wins. With a side and rear wall to choose from and neighbours further away, the sound calculation is easier to pass, and planning permission is not normally needed.

Read the detail

Placement is where a detached house wins. To be permitted development in England, the outdoor unit must pass the MCS 020(a) sound calculation, which sets a limit of 37 dB at a point one metre outside the nearest window or door of a habitable room in a neighbouring home. On a detached plot the installer has a side wall and a rear wall to choose from, the nearest neighbouring window is usually further away than on a semi, and the unit can be sited away from the bedrooms so you do not hear it either. Manufacturers rate heat pumps of this size at a sound power from the low 50s to the low 60s dB(A), and a 3-bed detached house rarely needs an acoustic enclosure. Anti-vibration feet under the unit stop hum travelling into the wall. The best spot is usually the side wall nearest the cylinder.

Planning permission is not normally needed. Since 29 May 2025, an air source heat pump on a house in England is permitted development if it passes the MCS 020(a) calculation, its outdoor unit is no larger than 1.5 cubic metres, there are no more than two on a detached house, it is not on a pitched roof or above the ground floor on a wall facing a road, and the house is not listed. In a conservation area it cannot go on a wall or roof facing a road, or nearer the road than the house. The old rule that the unit had to sit at least one metre from the boundary no longer applies. Our planning permission guide covers the exceptions and the rules in Wales and Scotland.

Getting quotes: what to check

Get three MCS quotes and compare the design, not the headline price. Check the heat loss calculation, a flow temperature of 50°C or lower, the radiator schedule, the grant rate and, on oil, who removes the tank.

Read the detail

Get three quotes from MCS certified installers, and compare them on the design rather than the headline price. Each quote should include:

  • A room-by-room heat loss calculation with the design outdoor temperature stated.
  • The design flow temperature, ideally 50°C or lower, and the seasonal performance expected at it.
  • A radiator schedule listing all 10 to 12 emitters, which ones change, their new sizes, and whether that work is in the price.
  • The cylinder size and where it will go.
  • An MCS 020(a) sound calculation for the outdoor unit position, with the nearest neighbouring window marked.
  • The warranty on the unit and on the installation, and who you call when something fails.
  • Confirmation that the installer will claim the Boiler Upgrade Scheme at the £7,500 or £9,000 rate and deduct it from the invoice.
  • On oil, who removes the tank and what that costs, because it is often left off the quote.

Installation usually takes several days for a detached house, longer when most radiators are swapped or the oil tank comes out. Find installers through the MCS installer search, or use the quote form on this page to ask up to three MCS certified installers covering your postcode to contact you. Frequently asked questions

How much does a heat pump cost for a 3-bed detached house?

£11,000 to £15,500 installed for a typical 9.5 kW job, the middle half of what installers recorded under the Boiler Upgrade Scheme, including the cylinder, labour and VAT. After the £7,500 BUS grant you pay £3,500 to £8,000. Homes heated by oil or LPG with no mains gas get £9,000 until 31 March 2027, which leaves £2,000 to £6,500. Radiator upgrades can add to that where they are needed.

What size heat pump does a 3-bed detached house need?

About 9.5 kW for a typical 3-bed detached house, 8 kW for a well insulated one and 10.5 kW for a poorly insulated one, on our model. That is a little above a semi, because of the fourth exposed wall. The installer's heat loss calculation decides. Insulate first to bring the size down.

How much does a heat pump cost to run in a 3-bed detached house?

£750 a year on a heat pump tariff and £997 on standard electricity, against £932 for gas and £1,400 for oil at 11.3p per kWh, the BoilerJuice UK average on 25 September 2026, at the October to December 2026 price cap with 10,530 kWh of heat. See our running costs guide.

Which heat pump is best for a 3-bed detached house?

Any correctly sized unit of around 8 to 12 kW from Vaillant, Mitsubishi, Daikin, Samsung, Grant or Viessmann can do the job, and the Octopus Cosy 9 suits the well insulated end. Choose on the design, the flow temperature and the seasonal performance in the quote rather than the brand.

Is a heat pump worth it for a 3-bed detached house on oil?

Usually, yes. With oil at 11.3p per kWh, the BoilerJuice UK average of 116.20p a litre on 25 September 2026, oil heating costs about £1,400 a year against £750 for a heat pump on a heat pump tariff, a saving of £650. Oil prices move, so that saving will too. The £9,000 grant for oil and LPG homes with no mains gas takes the whole job to £2,000 to £6,500 until 31 March 2027. Compare with our boiler vs heat pump tool.

Is ground source worth it for a 3-bed detached house?

Rarely on cost alone. Under the scheme the middle half of ground source installations cost £18,136 to £42,110 in April to June 2026, against £11,000 to £15,500 for a typical 3-bed detached air source job. A detached garden makes it practical, with a borehole or a ground loop, so it suits houses already digging for an extension or with nowhere acceptable for an outdoor unit.
