Best Electricity Tariffs for Heat Pump Owners in the UK

The short answer

  • About £232 a year off a 3-bed semi's heating with Cosy Octopus, the best open tariff, or about £308 for a 4-bed detached
  • Between 12 and 25 per cent off your heating cost on a heat pump tariff, about £109 to £232 a year for a 3-bed semi
  • No evening peak on EDF Heat Pump Tracker, with two three-hour windows at 10p below your regional standard rate
  • A smart meter sending half-hourly readings is needed for every heat pump tariff, and your supplier fits it free
TariffsOur pickSavingsOff-peak tipsSwitchingFAQs

Five suppliers run an electricity tariff built for heat pump homes that is open to new customers on 25 September 2026, and the best of them cuts the heating bill of a 3-bed semi by about £232 a year against the standard variable rate, or about £308 for a 4-bed detached house. The tariff you choose is the single biggest factor in whether a heat pump costs more or less to run than gas. This guide compares the open tariffs on their published rates and windows, works out what each one saves, and says which to pick.

Heat pump tariffs open in September 2026

They charge less in set hours of the day, and all need a smart meter. Cosy Octopus is about 13p off-peak against the 26.32p standard cap rate, and rates vary by region.

Our recommendation

Cosy Octopus for most homes, EDF Heat Pump Tracker if you want no peak. Cosy gives eight cheap hours in three windows with no exit fee, but its 4pm to 7pm peak costs extra.

Read the detail

For most heat pump owners, Cosy Octopus is still the tariff to beat. Eight cheap hours a day in three windows, no tie to any heat pump brand, no exit fee, and a 1pm to 4pm window that lets you heat the cylinder and warm the house in the mildest part of the day, when the heat pump is at its most efficient. The catch is the 4pm to 7pm peak, 50 per cent above the day rate. A home that cannot coast through those three hours on stored warmth loses part of the saving.

If you would rather have no peak penalty at all, EDF Heat Pump Tracker is the simplest option on the market: two three-hour windows at 10p below your regional standard rate, the standard rate the rest of the time, no exit fee and the same standing charge as EDF's standard tariff. It saves less than Cosy on paper but it cannot punish you.

E.ON Next Pumped suits homes that heat mostly overnight and have a large hot water cylinder, because its cheapest band runs from 10pm to 6am. British Gas Heat Power has the longest cheap windows, ten hours a day at half price, but British Gas only shows the rate it halves when you ask for a quote. Intelligent Octopus Go has the lowest off-peak rate of all at 8p, but you need a compatible electric car or charger to join, so it is an EV tariff that happens to suit a heat pump. Octopus Agile can do well when a smart controller shifts the heating around the price, and costs about the same as the standard rate when nothing does.

How much each tariff saves

Cosy Octopus saves most, £232 (25%) a year on a 3-bed semi. It costs £705 against £937 on the standard rate; the other tariffs save £109 to £178, and Agile with no controls saves only £7.

Read the detail

The table shows what a 3-bed semi with 9,900 kWh of annual heat demand pays for heating on each tariff, with the heat pump running at 2.78, the median measured in the Electrification of Heat trial for DESNZ. That is 3,561 kWh of electricity a year. We assume the share of heating that moves into the cheap windows scales with how many cheap hours the tariff offers: 60 per cent with eight hours, 55 per cent with seven, 50 per cent with six and 45 per cent with five. Where a tariff has a 4pm to 7pm peak, 10 per cent of heating lands in it. Standing charges are left out. Our calculators use this Cosy Octopus costing for a heat pump tariff, 19.8p per kWh, so they give the same £705 a year for this semi. A home that kept all its heating out of the 4pm to 7pm peak would pay about 18.4p per kWh instead. British Gas Heat Power and Intelligent Octopus Go are not costed, because their unit rates are only shown in a quote.

You need a smart meter

Every heat pump tariff on this page, and Agile, needs a smart meter that sends half-hourly readings: a second generation (SMETS2) meter, or a first generation one your supplier can connect to. Your supplier arranges the installation and, under government rules, does not charge you for it. Book it before you apply, because the tariff cannot go live without it.

What changed in 2026

OVO Heat Pump Plus closed and Cosy Octopus added a 12 month fix. Electricity carries no VAT from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027, so compare tariffs on their October prices.

Read the detail

OVO Heat Pump Plus closed. OVO stopped taking new customers on Heat Pump Plus in late November 2025, and its staff confirmed on the OVO Forum that the tariff has not been available since 1 February 2026. OVO lists no replacement heat pump tariff, so OVO customers with a heat pump need to switch supplier for a heat pump rate.

Cosy Octopus has a new 12 month fix. Its three windows still total eight hours a day, and a 12 month fixed version sits alongside the variable one; the current fix went on sale on 22 September 2026. On 25 September its rates were well above the variable tariff's, so compare both on the day you switch.

Octopus is not the only choice. EDF, E.ON Next, British Gas and ScottishPower all run a heat pump tariff, and none of them requires a particular heat pump brand. Good Energy still has a heat pump tariff page, but on 25 September 2026 its tariff list showed every heat pump tariff as closed, including the August 2026 fix (15p off-peak from 5am to 9am and 1pm to 4pm, £75 exit fee). Ask Good Energy before relying on it.

VAT on electricity is zero from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027. Gas keeps its 5 per cent rate. That is why the October cap rate of 26.32p is only 0.8 per cent above the summer rate of 26.11p, and why you should compare tariffs on their October prices rather than on figures published in the summer.

How to shift heating to off-peak

Heat in the cheap windows and coast through the peak. Warm the house from 4am, heat the cylinder in the afternoon window, and let a smart thermostat schedule it, which is the only sensible way on Agile.

Read the detail

The saving from any of these tariffs depends on how much of your heating you can move into the cheap hours. These are the habits that make the difference.

Warm the house before the window closes. On Cosy Octopus and EDF Heat Pump Tracker, run the heating from 4am so the house is up to temperature by 7am. A well-insulated home then holds most of that warmth through the morning. The better your loft insulation and draught-proofing, the longer it lasts.

Heat the cylinder in the afternoon. The 1pm to 4pm window on Cosy, EDF and British Gas, and ScottishPower's 11am to 4pm window, fall in the warmest part of the day, when the heat pump gets the most heat from each unit of electricity. Set the hot water to heat then, and size the cylinder so it carries the household through to the next window.

Coast through the peak. On tariffs with a 4pm to 7pm peak, set the thermostat one or two degrees lower for those three hours. In a home with decent insulation the drop is barely noticeable, and it avoids paying about 40p to 50p a unit.

Let a controller do it. A smart thermostat that knows your tariff windows schedules all of this for you, and on Agile it is the only sensible way to run a heat pump.

Use thermal mass. Solid floors, brick walls, big radiators and a large cylinder all store heat. Homes with plenty of it can heat hard in the cheap hours and coast through the expensive ones. Homes with little of it should favour a tariff with no evening peak, such as EDF's.

Standing charges and exit fees

No exit fee on Cosy, EDF or E.ON, but standing charges vary. EDF Heat Pump Tracker and E.ON Next Pumped charge 42.92p a day in London up to 67.66p in North Wales and Merseyside.

Read the detail

Unit rates get the attention, but the daily standing charge is paid every day of the year regardless. From 1 October, EDF Heat Pump Tracker charges 42.92p a day in London up to 67.66p in North Wales and Merseyside, the same as EDF's standard tariff, and E.ON Next Pumped charges the same amounts. Cosy Octopus charged 42p a day in London and 51p in the East Midlands on 25 September, including VAT. Cosy Octopus (both versions), EDF Heat Pump Tracker and E.ON Next Pumped have no exit fee, and ScottishPower lets you move between its own tariffs without one.

Switching tariff

Octopus, EDF and ScottishPower make you join a standard tariff first. You move across once the smart meter connects, usually around 14 days, while E.ON Next lets you sign up directly.

Read the detail

Octopus, EDF and ScottishPower ask new customers to join a standard tariff first and move to the heat pump tariff once the smart meter is connected; Octopus and EDF say connecting to the meter usually takes around 14 days. E.ON Next lets new and existing customers sign up to Next Pumped directly, but it needs monthly Direct Debit and an online account, and is not open to Smart Pay As You Go. British Gas Heat Power is a fixed tariff for British Gas electricity customers who pay by credit, on monthly or quarterly bills by Direct Debit, not by prepayment. If you are part way through a fixed deal with another supplier, check its exit fee before you move.

Combining a heat pump tariff with solar panels

Solar covers spring and summer days and the tariff covers winter. The 1pm to 4pm windows on Cosy and EDF overlap the sunniest hours, and a battery lets you run the evening peak on stored power.

Read the detail

With solar panels, the heat pump can run partly on your own solar power on spring and summer days, and the tariff supplies cheaper power for the winter. The 1pm to 4pm windows on Cosy and EDF overlap the sunniest part of the day, so on a bright afternoon solar power and the cheap rate work together. Add a battery and you can charge it in the cheap window as well, then run the evening peak from stored power. The Energy Saving Trust reports that combining a heat pump with solar panels, a battery and a time of use tariff can cut bills substantially. The solar payback guide has the full figures, and your smart meter's in-home display shows what you are spending as you go.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best electricity tariff for a heat pump?

Cosy Octopus for most homes: eight cheap hours a day in three windows, no brand tie-in, no exit fee, and the biggest saving in our model at about £232 a year for a 3-bed semi. EDF Heat Pump Tracker is the best choice if you want cheap windows with no evening peak.

How much can I save with a heat pump tariff?

Between 12 and 25 per cent of your heating cost on the dedicated heat pump tariffs we could cost, depending on the tariff and how much heating you can move into the cheap hours. For a 3-bed semi that is about £109 to £232 a year, and for a 4-bed detached house about £308 on Cosy Octopus. Use the heat pump calculator for your own property.

Do I need a smart meter for a heat pump tariff?

Yes. Every heat pump tariff on this page needs a smart meter sending half-hourly readings. Your supplier arranges the installation and does not charge you for it, and the tariff cannot start until the meter is connected.

Do I need an Octopus heat pump to get Cosy Octopus?

No. Cosy Octopus is open to homes with an air or ground source heat pump, an electric boiler or electric radiators, whoever installed them.

Can I use Octopus Agile with a heat pump?

Yes, but only with controls that move the heating away from the expensive half hours. In our model, Agile with automatic controls saved about £167 a year on a 3-bed semi at August and September 2026 prices; with the heating spread evenly across the day it cost about the same as the standard rate.

Is Economy 7 still worth it for a heat pump?

It can come close if your heat pump can do most of its work in the seven night hours. On Octopus's Economy 7 rates it saves about £145 a year on a 3-bed semi in our model, against £109 to £232 on the dedicated heat pump tariffs. The catch is a day rate of about 32p for anything left over, and night hours set by your meter rather than chosen for a heat pump.
