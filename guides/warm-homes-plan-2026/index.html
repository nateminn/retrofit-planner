Warm Homes Plan 2026: Everything You Need to Know

The Warm Homes Plan is the government's £15 billion programme of public investment to upgrade up to 5 million homes and lift up to a million families out of fuel poverty by 2030. Published on 21 January 2026, it brings the supplier schemes, ECO4 and the Great British Insulation Scheme, to a close and puts the money into grants for low-income homes, low and zero interest loans, and the Boiler Upgrade Scheme. This guide explains what is confirmed, what you can apply for now, and what is still being set up.

What happens to ECO4 and GBIS

GBIS ended on 31 March 2026 as planned. ECO4 has been extended by nine months to 31 December 2026, without any increase in its delivery targets, which also gives time to put right poor installations. The plan says supplier obligations did not represent strong value for money, so there will be no successor supplier scheme.

This means the transition period is now. If you qualify for ECO4, apply now: the work has to be installed by 31 December 2026. In England, the Warm Homes: Local Grant is already open through councils, with published rules: household income below £36,000, a listed means-tested benefit, or an eligible postcode, and a home rated D to G.

Apply for ECO4 while it lasts

GBIS closed on 31 March 2026. ECO4 closes on 31 December 2026 and still provides free or part funded insulation and heating upgrades. Do not wait for the merged low-income scheme planned from 2027/28; apply for what is available today. Use our grant eligibility checker.

Key commitments

Up to 5 million home upgrades by 2030. The plan aims to upgrade up to 5 million homes with insulation, clean heating, and renewable energy, and to lift up to a million families out of fuel poverty.

Triple rooftop solar. The plan aims to put solar panels on up to 3 million more homes by 2030. Combined with battery storage, this aims to reduce household electricity costs and grid dependence.

£7,500 heat pump grant continues. The Boiler Upgrade Scheme is funded to 2029/30 with a total budget of £2.7 billion, £400 million of it in 2026 to 2027. Air-to-air heat pumps now qualify for a £2,500 grant, and until 31 March 2027 homes heated by oil or LPG with no mains gas get £9,000.

Low and zero interest loans. £2 billion backs a new consumer loan offer, delivered through banks and other lenders under the Warm Homes Loan Scheme for owner occupied and privately rented homes. The first window for lenders to join closed in July 2026 and another is planned later in 2026, so the loans are not yet something you can apply for directly. Check back before committing to paying cash.

Tighter landlord standards. Privately rented homes will need to meet EPC band C by October 2030, unless the property has a valid exemption. This replaces the existing EPC E minimum.

What it means for you

If you are on a low income or benefits

You are the primary target. In England the Warm Homes: Local Grant provides free upgrades through your local council, and ECO4 runs until 31 December 2026, so apply for both now rather than waiting for the merged scheme planned from 2027/28. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland run their own schemes, which our grant checker covers.

If you are a homeowner not on benefits

GBIS, the insulation scheme with no benefits test, closed on 31 March 2026. The £7,500 BUS grant for heat pumps is open to every owner in England and Wales regardless of income, and in England the Warm Homes: Local Grant has an income route for households under £36,000. The new low and zero interest loans will come through lenders once they have signed up.

If you are a landlord

Start planning now. Rented homes will need EPC band C by October 2030, unless exempt. Read our EPC for landlords guide and use our EPC improvement planner.

If you are a social tenant

Your housing association should be applying to the Warm Homes: Social Housing Fund on your behalf. Contact them to ask what upgrade plans are in place for your home.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Warm Homes Plan?

The government's £15 billion programme of public investment, published on 21 January 2026, to upgrade up to 5 million homes and lift up to a million families out of fuel poverty by 2030. It includes grants for low-income homes, the Boiler Upgrade Scheme, new low and zero interest loans, and higher standards for rented homes. It brings the supplier schemes ECO4 and GBIS to a close.

Does the Warm Homes Plan replace ECO4?

Yes. ECO4 ends on 31 December 2026 with no successor supplier scheme. Support for low-income households moves to government grants: the Warm Homes: Local Grant in England now, and from 2027/28 a single low-income scheme the government intends to create. Apply for ECO4 now while it exists.

Can I get a loan for energy improvements?

Not yet directly. The plan puts £2 billion behind low and zero interest loans, delivered through lenders under the Warm Homes Loan Scheme. The first lender window closed in July 2026 and another is planned later in 2026, so check back before paying cash for a heat pump, solar panels or insulation.
