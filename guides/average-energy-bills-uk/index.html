Average Energy Bills UK 2026: What Households Actually Pay

The average UK household pays approximately £1,723 per year for gas and electricity combined at the Ofgem price cap for October to December 2026. But averages hide huge variation. A 2 bed flat pays around £1,414 while a 5 bed detached house pays around £2,672. Here is the full breakdown.

Why your bill might be higher than average

Poor insulation. A poorly insulated 3 bed semi uses around 31% more gas than a well-insulated one. That is the difference between an £854 and a £1,081 gas bill. Our energy bills by EPC rating guide shows what each band typically pays. Insulation is the single biggest factor in your energy bill, and many households qualify for free insulation through ECO4.

Old inefficient boiler. A 20-year-old boiler might operate at 70% to 80% efficiency versus 90% to 92% for a modern condensing boiler. That 15% to 20% efficiency gap translates directly into higher bills. A heat pump or new boiler can close this gap.

High thermostat setting. Heat loss rises with the gap between indoor and outdoor temperature, so every degree on the thermostat adds to the heating bill. Heating to a comfortable 18°C to 21°C, rather than higher, keeps it down.

Standard variable tariff. If you have never switched supplier or tariff, you are likely on the most expensive rate. Fixed and smart tariffs are sometimes priced below the cap, so it is worth checking.

How to reduce your energy bills

The most impactful actions, ranked by potential savings:

1. Insulate your home. In government measurements of real homes, those that had cavity wall insulation fitted used a median 12.0% less gas afterwards, and those that had cavity and loft insulation together 8.4% less (DESNZ NEED, 2026). Many households get it free through ECO4 until 31 December 2026.

2. Switch to a better tariff. Check comparison sites for fixed-rate deals below the price cap. Smart tariffs with time-of-use pricing can save money if you can shift usage to off-peak hours.

3. Turn down your thermostat. A degree lower on the thermostat cuts the heating bill, and heating only the rooms you use cuts it further. Smart heating controls (£150 to £400) automate this and add about 3 EPC points in our model.

4. Consider a heat pump. For a 3 bed semi on a heat pump tariff, a heat pump costs about 40% less to run than oil or LPG heating and about 75% less than direct electric heating. The Boiler Upgrade Scheme grant of £7,500, or £9,000 when it replaces oil or LPG, brings the median installed cost of £12,700 for a 3 bed semi down to about £5,200 or £3,700. On gas the saving is smaller: about £240 a year on a heat pump tariff, while on a standard tariff it costs about £60 a year more.

5. Add solar panels. A 4 kW solar system costs £5,000 to £7,000 and brings in about £490 to £560 a year in bill savings and export payments on a south facing roof, paying for itself in about 11 to 12 years. Installations are zero rated for VAT until 31 March 2027.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average energy bill in the UK in 2026?

Approximately £1,723 per year for gas and electricity combined at the October to December 2026 cap, based on Ofgem typical consumption values of 2,500 kWh of electricity and 9,500 kWh of gas. Actual bills range from £1,265 for a 1 bed flat to around £2,672 for a 5 bed detached house.

What is the average gas bill in the UK in 2026?

Approximately £865 per year based on Ofgem typical consumption of 9,500 kWh. Gas costs 7.97p per kWh at the October to December 2026 price cap, plus a 29.68p daily standing charge.

What is the average electricity bill in the UK in 2026?

Approximately £858 per year based on Ofgem typical consumption of 2,500 kWh. Electricity costs 26.32p per kWh at the October to December 2026 price cap, plus a 54.83p daily standing charge. Homes with heat pumps or electric vehicles consume more.

How much does a 3 bed house cost to heat in 2026?

A 3 bed semi costs approximately £1,710 to £1,930 per year for gas and electricity. Well-insulated homes are at the lower end. Improving insulation from poor to good takes the heating part down by about 20%, which is roughly 12% off the whole bill once electricity is counted.
