ECO4 Scheme Explained: How to Get Free Insulation and Heating in the UK

The short answer

  • Free or part funded insulation and heating for eligible households, closing on 31 December 2026, and the work must be installed by then
  • D to G if you own, or E to G if you rent privately, where someone in the household claims a qualifying benefit
  • Below £31,000 gross household income can qualify through a council referral under ECO4 Flex

The ECO4 scheme funds free or part funded insulation and heating upgrades for eligible households in England, Scotland and Wales, and it closes on 31 December 2026. By the end of April 2026 it had installed about 1.04 million measures in 306,600 homes, including ECO3 interim delivery and measures carried over from ECO3. You can qualify if you own your home and it is rated D, E, F or G, or rent privately and it is rated E, F or G, and someone in the household claims a qualifying benefit. A home rated C or above does not qualify. Social housing rated E to G qualifies without any benefit, and councils can refer some other low income households. This guide explains who qualifies, what is covered, how to apply, and what to watch out for.

What is ECO4?

A scheme where energy suppliers fund upgrades for low income homes. Ofgem runs it in Great Britain, it puts insulation before heating, and it runs until 31 December 2026.

Read the detail

ECO4 stands for Energy Company Obligation 4. It is a government scheme in Great Britain that legally requires the larger energy suppliers to fund energy efficiency improvements in low-income and fuel-poor households. Ofgem runs it and publishes the list of suppliers taking part. It does not run in Northern Ireland.

The scheme started in April 2022, following ECO3, and after a nine-month extension confirmed by the government in January 2026 it runs until 31 December 2026. ECO4 takes a "fabric first" approach, meaning insulation comes before heating system replacements.

ECO4 works on the whole home rather than one measure at a time, and the assessor designs a package to lift the EPC rating. Use our EPC improvement planner to see what upgrades your property needs.

Who qualifies for ECO4?

Owners of homes rated D to G, or private tenants in E to G homes, where someone claims a qualifying benefit. Social housing rated E to G qualifies on the property alone, and councils can refer households below £31,000.

Read the detail

There are three routes to eligibility: the benefits route for owners and private tenants, the social housing route, and the ECO4 Flex route through your council.

Benefits route

You qualify if you or someone in your household receives one of these benefits and your home has the right EPC rating: D, E, F or G if you own it, or E, F or G if you rent it privately.

Universal Credit, Pension Credit (Guarantee Credit or Savings Credit), Income Support, income-based Jobseeker's Allowance (JSA), income-related Employment and Support Allowance (ESA), Housing Benefit, or Child Benefit. Child Benefit only counts below a household income limit: £19,900 to £34,500 for a single parent and £27,500 to £42,000 for a couple, rising with the number of children. Tax credits ended in April 2025.

A private tenant needs the landlord's permission for the work. Social housing rated E, F or G can qualify on the property alone, with no benefit needed; your council or housing association arranges it.

ECO4 Flex route

If you do not receive qualifying benefits, your local council can refer you through ECO4 Flex. The most common route is a gross household income below £31,000. The others are a set of low income proxies, a referral on health grounds, and targeting set by the council. The EPC rules are the same as the benefits route, D to G for owners and E to G for private tenants, except that the low income proxy route needs E to G for owners too. Flex is for private housing only, so social housing is not covered by it.

ECO4 Flex is worth exploring even if you think you will not qualify through the main route. Contact your council's energy or housing team to ask. Some councils are proactive about identifying eligible households, while others require you to approach them.

Check eligibility before paying for insulation

If there is any chance you qualify for ECO4, check before paying for insulation yourself. The Energy Saving Trust puts professional loft insulation at about £750 for a bare loft, or you can do it yourself, but ECO4 can cover it with professional installation. The same applies to solid wall insulation, which costs about £12,000 internally or £15,000 externally for a typical 3-bed semi at full price.

What measures does ECO4 cover?

Insulation first, then heating, as a whole house package. It covers loft, cavity and solid wall insulation, boilers, heating controls and, in some homes, heat pumps and solar panels; windows and doors are rarely funded.

How to apply for ECO4

Through your council or a participating energy supplier, as there is no government form. An assessor surveys the home, the installer does the work, and you should never pay an upfront fee.

Read the detail

There is no single government application form. The process works through installers and energy suppliers.

Step 1: Check your eligibility. Use our grant eligibility checker or confirm that you receive one of the qualifying benefits listed above, and look up your rating on the GOV.UK EPC register. If unsure, contact your energy supplier or local council.

Step 2: Contact your council or an energy supplier taking part. GOV.UK suggests asking your council whether it is taking part, or contacting a participating energy supplier directly; Ofgem lists them. Installers and agents may also contact you. If you are not sure an offer is genuine, check with your council or the supplier they say they represent.

Step 3: Property assessment. A qualified assessor visits your home to survey its current condition, measure insulation levels, check the heating system, and determine which measures are needed. They produce a retrofit assessment based on PAS 2035 standards.

Step 4: Installation. Once approved, the installer carries out the work. This may involve multiple trades (insulation, heating, ventilation) and can take several days to several weeks depending on the scope. The installer manages the full process.

Step 5: Post-installation check. After completion, the installer should arrange a post-installation inspection and provide documentation including any warranties. A new EPC should be commissioned to confirm the improvement.

Watch out for ECO4 scams

Never pay an upfront fee for ECO4 work. The scheme is free for eligible households. Be cautious of cold callers or door knockers claiming to offer "government-funded" improvements and asking for deposits. Legitimate ECO4 installers will verify your eligibility before starting work and will not ask for payment. Check any installer on the TrustMark register.

ECO4 for landlords

Yes, if the home is rated E, F or G and the tenant qualifies. The tenant needs a qualifying benefit or a council referral, and the landlord must consent and may be asked to contribute.

Read the detail

ECO4 is aimed at owner-occupiers, private tenants and social housing. A privately rented home qualifies if it is rated E, F or G and the tenant receives a qualifying benefit or is referred by the council. The landlord must consent to the work and may be asked to contribute to the cost, depending on the installer and the measures involved.

For landlords facing minimum EPC requirements, which the Warm Homes Plan raises to band C by October 2030, ECO4 can cut the cost of the work. If your tenant qualifies, the scheme could fund solid wall insulation or a boiler upgrade that would otherwise cost thousands.

Landlords can also access the separate Boiler Upgrade Scheme for heat pump installation, and benefit from 0% VAT on insulation and renewable energy measures until 31 March 2027.

ECO4 Flex: the council referral route

Your council can refer you without a qualifying benefit. The most common route is a gross household income below £31,000; the others are low income proxies, a health referral and council targeting.

Read the detail

ECO4 Flex was introduced to help households who are fuel-poor but do not receive qualifying benefits. Under Flex, your local council can declare you eligible through one of four routes set by Ofgem:

Route 1, household income: Gross annual household income below £31,000, adding up every adult who lives there. The threshold is the same whatever the size of the home or where it is.

Route 2, low income proxies: Meeting at least two of a set of low income indicators, such as living in one of the most deprived areas, getting a low income Council Tax reduction, or having a child eligible for free school meals. On this route an owned home must be rated E to G.

Route 3, health referral: A referral on health grounds, for a household member with a condition made worse by living in a cold home.

Route 4, council targeting: Targeting designed by the council for its own area.

Whatever the route, the home must be rated D to G if you own it (E to G on the proxy route), or E to G if you rent privately, and social housing is not covered by Flex. Councils decide whether to take part and how actively they promote it, so it is always worth asking your council's housing or environmental health team.

Quality standards and what to expect

All work must meet PAS 2035 and use TrustMark registered installers. Quality still varies, so check that damp and ventilation are assessed, and take unresolved problems to TrustMark's dispute resolution service.

Read the detail

All ECO4 work must comply with PAS 2035, the UK standard for retrofitting domestic buildings. This means a qualified retrofit assessor must survey your property before work begins, a retrofit coordinator must oversee the project, and all installers must be TrustMark registered.

In practice, quality varies. Some installers deliver excellent work. Others cut corners, particularly on ventilation (which must be addressed when insulating a property to prevent condensation problems). Here is what to look for:

Before work starts: You should receive a clear explanation of what will be installed, how long it will take, and what disruption to expect. The assessor should check for damp, asbestos risk, and ventilation needs. If they do not mention ventilation, ask. See our guide on loft insulation lifespan to judge material quality.

During installation: Workers should protect your home, clean up daily, and communicate about progress. Loft insulation should reach 270mm. Cavity wall insulation should fill all cavities evenly. Wall insulation should include a vapour control layer where required.

After completion: You should receive a handover pack with details of what was installed, any warranties, and care instructions. A new EPC should be arranged. If you are not satisfied with the work, raise concerns immediately with the installer and, if unresolved, contact TrustMark's dispute resolution service. A thermal imaging camera can confirm insulation coverage after installation.

What happens after ECO4 ends?

There will be no ECO5. ECO4 closes on 31 December 2026 and support moves to council run grants, so apply now: the work must be installed by then, not just approved.

Read the detail

ECO4 closes on 31 December 2026. The Warm Homes Plan, published in January 2026, brings supplier obligations to a close, so there will be no ECO5. Support for low income households moves to government grants run through councils: the Warm Homes: Local Grant in England now, and from 2027/28 a single low income scheme that the government intends to create by merging it with the Warm Homes: Social Housing Fund.

If you think you might qualify, apply now rather than waiting. The work has to be installed by 31 December 2026, not just approved, and the council run schemes that follow do not offer identical coverage. Insulation installed under ECO4 keeps working long after the scheme ends.

Other grants and funding

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme, 0% VAT and the Warm Home Discount are also available. BUS gives £7,500 towards a heat pump, or £9,000 for oil or LPG homes until 31 March 2027, and the discount is £150.

Read the detail

ECO4 is not the only funding available. Depending on your circumstances, you may also qualify for:

Boiler Upgrade Scheme (BUS): £7,500 towards an air source heat pump, or £9,000 until 31 March 2027 for homes heated by oil or LPG with no mains gas, for owners in England and Wales replacing fossil fuel or electric heating. Read our full BUS guide. This can be combined with ECO4 insulation.

0% VAT on energy efficiency measures: Insulation, solar panels, and heat pumps installed in residential properties qualify for 0% VAT until 31 March 2027. This applies even if you are paying for the work yourself.

Warm Home Discount: A £150 discount on your winter electricity bill. In England and Wales it goes to households on Universal Credit, Housing Benefit, income-related ESA or Pension Credit, and Scotland has its own rules. It is separate from ECO4 and usually applied automatically by your supplier.

Use our grant eligibility checker to see all available funding for your situation. If you are considering a heat pump alongside ECO4 insulation, our heat pump calculator shows the combined cost after all grants.

Making the most of ECO4 improvements

Get a new EPC, learn your heating controls and check your bills. Cheap DIY extras such as draught-proofing and secondary glazing tackle the heat loss ECO4 did not cover.

Read the detail

Once your home has been insulated and upgraded through ECO4, a few additional steps maximise the benefit.

Get a new EPC. The improvements will significantly change your rating. A new EPC documents this, which matters if you sell or rent the property. Use our EPC improvement planner to estimate your new rating.

Learn your heating controls. Many households have the right equipment but use it inefficiently. If you received new heating controls, spend time understanding the programmer and thermostat.

Monitor your energy usage. Compare your bills before and after to confirm the savings are materialising. Check our average energy bills guide to benchmark against typical households.

Address remaining heat loss. ECO4 may not cover everything. Draught-proofing, secondary glazing, and chimney balloons are cheap additions you can do yourself to squeeze out further savings. Read our guide to improving your EPC rating for the full list of options.

Frequently asked questions

What is the ECO4 scheme?

ECO4 is the Energy Company Obligation, a government scheme in Great Britain that requires the larger energy suppliers to fund insulation and heating upgrades for eligible households, at no cost or part cost to them. It runs until 31 December 2026. Check our grant eligibility tool to see if you qualify.

Who qualifies for free insulation under ECO4?

Owners of homes rated D to G, or private tenants in homes rated E to G, where someone claims Universal Credit, Pension Credit, Income Support, income-based JSA, income-related ESA, Housing Benefit, or Child Benefit below an income limit. Social housing rated E to G qualifies without a benefit. Councils can also refer households through ECO4 Flex, most often on a gross household income below £31,000.

Can I get ECO4 with an EPC rating of C?

Not in its own right. ECO4 needs a rating of D to G if you own the home, or E to G if you rent it, so a home rated C or above does not qualify through the benefits, social housing or council routes. The one exception is a flat in a block where a qualifying neighbour is getting cavity or solid wall insulation or a heat network connection, which can be extended to it. The Boiler Upgrade Scheme has no EPC band requirement if you want a heat pump.

What does ECO4 cover?

Loft insulation, cavity wall insulation, solid wall insulation, underfloor and room-in-roof insulation, boiler repairs and replacements, heating controls, first-time central heating, and in some homes air source heat pumps or solar panels. The scheme takes a "fabric first" approach, prioritising insulation before heating.

How do I apply for ECO4?

There is no single government form. Contact your council to ask whether it is taking part, or a participating energy supplier directly; Ofgem lists them. Your council can also refer you through ECO4 Flex. Use our grant checker as a starting point.

When does ECO4 end?

ECO4 ends on 31 December 2026, and the work has to be installed by then, not just approved. The Warm Homes Plan brings supplier obligations to a close, so there is no successor supplier scheme. Apply now rather than waiting.
