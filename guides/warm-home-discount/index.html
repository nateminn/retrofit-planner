Warm Home Discount: How to Get £150 Off Your Energy Bill

The Warm Home Discount gives eligible households a £150 rebate on their electricity bill each winter. It reopens in October 2026 for winter 2026 to 2027. In England and Wales you qualify if, on 23 August 2026, you or your partner got Universal Credit, Housing Benefit, income-related Employment and Support Allowance or Pension Credit, and one of you was named on the electricity bill with a supplier in the scheme. Most people get it automatically. Last winter 5.52 million households received it through the automatic core groups, about 2.3 million more than the year before, and the government expects around six million this winter. This guide explains who qualifies, how to check, and what to do if you do not receive it.

Who qualifies

The rules differ between England and Wales, and Scotland. The scheme does not run in Northern Ireland, where the Affordable Warmth Scheme is the main help.

England and Wales: the core group (automatic)

You are in the core group for winter 2026 to 2027 if all of these applied on 23 August 2026: your electricity supplier was part of the scheme, you or your partner got one of the benefits below, and your name or your partner's was on the electricity bill. You do not need to apply. Your supplier applies the £150 to your bill.

Qualifying benefits in England and Wales

Since 2025 to 2026 there has been no separate "high cost to heat" test: every billpayer household on one of these benefits is in line for the rebate.

Scotland: core group and broader group

In Scotland the same 23 August 2026 date and bill rules apply, but the lists differ. You get the discount automatically if you or your partner get Pension Credit, get Universal Credit with an extra amount for a disability or health condition, or get a disability or pensioner premium with income-related ESA or a Support for Mortgage Interest loan. You are also paid automatically if you look after a child under 5 and are unemployed on Universal Credit, get income-related ESA, or have a Support for Mortgage Interest loan. Anyone else on a low income applies to their electricity supplier as part of the broader group, and each supplier sets its own criteria.

Suppliers match most households automatically using government benefit data. If you were eligible for last winter and did not get the discount by 31 March 2026, GOV.UK says to contact your electricity supplier first.

You or your partner must be named on the electricity bill

Suppliers rely on their records from 23 August 2026. Either of you can be the one getting the benefit, and either of you can be the named billpayer. The most common reason for missing out is not being named on the bill at all, for example after moving house. The discount is usually applied to your electricity account, though a supplier that provides both your gas and electricity may apply it to gas instead.

How the rebate works

The £150 is credited directly to your electricity account. You do not receive cash. If you pay by direct debit, you will see the credit on a statement over the winter. If you use a prepayment meter, your supplier will tell you how you get it, for example as a voucher to top up the meter. You receive it once per winter, not monthly, and it does not affect a Cold Weather Payment or Winter Fuel Payment.

You can receive the Warm Home Discount and also qualify for ECO4, the Boiler Upgrade Scheme or the Warm Homes: Local Grant. They are not mutually exclusive.

ECO4 closes on 31 December 2026. The work has to be finished by then, not just applied for, and installers fill up as the date gets closer. Government has confirmed there will be no successor supplier obligation: from 2027 the money moves to council run schemes under the Warm Homes Plan, which you apply for through your local authority rather than your energy supplier. If you think you qualify, start now. Check what you qualify for.

The Warm Home Discount is a bill rebate, not a home improvement. If you want to permanently reduce your energy bills rather than receiving £150 once per year, look into insulation and heating upgrades through ECO4: homes the government measured used a median 12.0% less gas after cavity wall insulation, about £105 a year for a typical 3-bed semi. What if you are not eligible

In England and Wales, if you do not receive one of the qualifying benefits you cannot get the Warm Home Discount. In Scotland, ask your supplier about its broader group. Other help does not need a benefit: in England the Warm Homes: Local Grant has an income route for households under £36,000 a year, and councils can refer households on under £31,000 into ECO4 until 31 December 2026. Cheap jobs such as draught-proofing and topping up loft insulation also help, and if you have a heat pump or electric heating it is worth checking for a better tariff.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get the Warm Home Discount?

In England and Wales, if you or your partner got Universal Credit, Housing Benefit, income-related ESA or Pension Credit on 23 August 2026 and one of you was named on the electricity bill, you should get it automatically for winter 2026 to 2027. In Scotland some groups are paid automatically and others apply to their supplier. The scheme reopens in October 2026.

When is the Warm Home Discount paid?

Over the winter, as a one-off £150 credit to your electricity account, not a monthly payment. Prepayment customers are told by their supplier how they will get it, for example as a voucher. If you were eligible and had not received it by the end of March, contact your supplier.

Can I get the Warm Home Discount and ECO4?

Yes. They are separate schemes. The Warm Home Discount is a bill rebate. ECO4 provides free or part funded home improvements until 31 December 2026. You can receive both.
