Heat Pump Cost for a Bungalow: Full 2026 Breakdown

The short answer

  • About £12,300 to £13,000 installed for a 2 or 3 bed bungalow, or £4,800 to £5,500 after the £7,500 grant
  • £9,000 grant for oil or LPG bungalows with no mains gas until March 2027, bringing it to £3,300 to £4,000
  • £737 a year to run a 3 bed bungalow on a heat pump tariff, £180 less than gas and £639 less than oil

An air source heat pump for a two or three bedroom bungalow costs about £12,300 to £13,000 installed, the median installers reported under the Boiler Upgrade Scheme in 2025/26 for a heat pump of that size, and most jobs cost £10,500 to £15,500. That price is the whole job as installers recorded it for the grant, cylinder, labour and VAT included; some homes also need larger radiators. The £7,500 Boiler Upgrade Scheme grant brings a typical bungalow to £4,800 to £5,500, and until March 2027 a bungalow heated by oil or LPG with no mains gas connection gets £9,000, which brings it to £3,300 to £4,000. At the October to December 2026 price cap a typical three bedroom bungalow costs £737 a year to run on a heat pump tariff, £180 less than gas and £639 less than oil at the 25 September 2026 oil price; on standard electricity it costs £980, which is £63 more than gas. A bungalow needs about the same size of heat pump as a 3-bed semi, and this guide explains why.

In 2025/26 the median across 30,590 installations was £13,021, and it rises with the size of the heat pump: £12,164 for a 6 to 8 kW unit, £12,686 for 8 to 10 kW and £13,943 for 10 to 12 kW (Boiler Upgrade Scheme statistics, August 2026, Table A1.3A). An installation also has fixed costs that do not shrink with the house, which is why a small bungalow costs almost as much as a larger one.

What size heat pump for a bungalow

About 7.5 kW for a 2 bed bungalow and 9.5 kW for a 3 bed. Top up the loft, about £600, before the survey, though on its own it rarely changes the heat pump size.

Read the detail

On our model a typical two bedroom bungalow needs a 7.5 kW air source heat pump and a three bedroom one 9.5 kW: 6.5 to 8 kW when well insulated and 8.5 to 10.5 kW when poorly insulated. Those sizes come from the house's annual heat demand spread over the heating season, and they are a guide. The number that decides your heat pump is the heat loss calculation: your MCS installer should survey every room and work out its heat loss on the design day, the coldest conditions the system is built for. A quote without a room-by-room calculation was sized by guesswork, and an oversized heat pump costs more and switches on and off more often, which wastes efficiency. Our heat pump sizing guide explains how the size is worked out.

Loft insulation is the biggest lever in a bungalow

In a two-storey house the walls are the largest heat-losing surface. In a bungalow the roof matters more: the loft floor of a 90 square metre bungalow is 90 square metres, about the same as all its external walls put together. Many bungalows still have a thin layer of insulation from decades ago, often squashed flat by storage. The Energy Saving Trust puts a professional top-up from 120 mm to 270 mm at about £600, or about £750 from nothing (updated 8 May 2026). The measured effect is smaller than most people expect: homes that had loft insulation fitted or topped up in 2023 used a median 3.2% less gas the following year, about 324 kWh or £26 at the October 2026 gas price (DESNZ NEED impact of measures, June 2026). A bungalow has more loft for its size than a two-storey house, so it matters more here, but on its own it rarely changes the size of heat pump. Do it before the survey so the installer designs for the improved house, and raise any storage on loft legs rather than squashing the insulation. Our DIY loft insulation guide covers the job, our loft insulation guide has the costs and savings, and mineral wool lasts 40 years or more.

Insulate the loft before the survey

A top-up costs about £600 fitted, according to the Energy Saving Trust, and less as a DIY job. Measured savings are modest, partly because many homes were under-heated before and take some of the gain as warmth rather than a lower bill. ECO4 may pay for it if the work is finished before the scheme closes on 31 December 2026, and in England households on a low income can ask their council about the Warm Homes: Local Grant; the Great British Insulation Scheme closed on 31 March 2026. Use our grant checker. A suspended timber floor is the next largest surface, so see underfloor insulation, and a 1930s or later bungalow with an unfilled cavity should look at cavity wall insulation.

Radiators, cylinder and pipework on one floor

A few radiators usually need upsizing, and short single floor pipe runs make the job quicker. With no landing, the cylinder needs a cupboard of at least 80 cm by 80 cm, not the loft.

Read the detail

A heat pump heats water to 40 to 50°C instead of the 65 to 75°C a boiler usually runs at, so radiators have to be larger to give out the same heat. In a two or three bedroom bungalow typically a few need upsizing, usually the living room, the hall and the main bedroom, and that work is part of the installed price rather than an extra. Bungalows have long, low walls under wide windows, so a double-panel radiator in the same slot solves most rooms. Our radiator sizing guide explains how installers decide which ones change.

Where the cylinder goes when everything is on the ground floor

You need a hot water cylinder sized to how much hot water the household uses, and the Energy Saving Trust says it will usually fit a cupboard of at least 80 cm by 80 cm (air source heat pumps, updated 16 July 2026). A two-storey house puts it in the landing airing cupboard; a bungalow has no landing, so the cylinder competes for living space. The usual answers are the hall airing cupboard many older bungalows have, the space the old boiler occupied, an integral garage or store, or a purpose-built cupboard. Slimline cylinders, narrower and taller than standard ones, suit a shallow cupboard. The loft is a last resort: 200 litres of water alone weighs 200 kg, ceiling joists were not sized for it, and the pipework needs heavy frost insulation. An oil bungalow usually has a cylinder and cupboard already; the cylinder still needs replacing with one made for a heat pump, with a larger coil, but the space problem is solved.

Short pipe runs make the job quicker

On one floor every pipe run is short and horizontal. The installer takes the flow and return through the wall, along the loft or under a suspended floor and into the cylinder cupboard without a single drop through a ceiling, which usually makes the job quicker and the pipework simpler than in a two-storey house. The exception is microbore, the 8 mm or 10 mm pipe fitted in many homes from the 1970s onwards, which restricts flow. Some systems can work with it: Octopus says its Cosy heat pumps work with microbore of 10 mm or larger. Ask for pipe sizes to be checked at the survey. Any pipe in the loft must be insulated as well as the loft floor is, or the first hard frost will find it.

Running costs: bungalow

£737 a year on a heat pump tariff, £180 less than gas and £639 less than oil. On standard electricity it costs £980, which is £63 more than gas.

Read the detail

These figures use the Ofgem price cap for 1 October to 31 December 2026: electricity 26.32p per kWh and gas 7.97p. Electricity carries no VAT from October 2026 to March 2027 while gas includes 5%, so if 5% VAT returns to electricity in April 2027 at the same underlying price, the heat pump figures rise by 5%, which would cut the heat pump tariff saving against gas to £143 a year. Oil is priced at 11.3p per kWh, the BoilerJuice UK average of 116.20p a litre on 25 September 2026, and LPG at 9.5p, the rates our calculator uses. Heating oil prices move from week to week, so treat the oil figures as a snapshot of that day rather than a fixed bill. Oil, LPG and the £9,000 grant

Oil or LPG bungalows with no mains gas get £9,000 until March 2027. That takes a typical 3 bed bungalow from £13,000 to £4,000, and low income households may get the work free through the council.

Read the detail

Many bungalows sit in villages and on the edges of towns where the gas main never arrived, so the off-gas rules are the ones most owners need. The Boiler Upgrade Scheme pays £7,500 towards an air source or ground source heat pump in England and Wales. Until March 2027 it pays £9,000 where the home is heated by oil or LPG and has no mains gas connection (GOV.UK), which brings a typical three bedroom bungalow from £13,000 to £4,000, or £2,000 to £6,500 across the middle half of jobs. Your MCS-certified installer applies, and since April 2026 the installer must take the grant off the price quoted to you. The scheme runs to 2030 and installations carry 0% VAT until March 2027. You qualify if you own the bungalow and the heat pump replaces fossil fuel or electric heating; since April 2026 you no longer need a valid EPC. A hybrid that keeps the oil boiler as backup does not qualify. What happens to the oil tank is your decision: most owners have it drained and removed by an OFTEC-registered technician. Read our BUS grant guide and the GOV.UK Boiler Upgrade Scheme page for the rules.

The Warm Homes: Local Grant for low income households

If you live in England, the bungalow has an EPC of D to G and household income is usually £36,000 a year or less, or you live in a qualifying postcode or someone in the household gets certain benefits, the Warm Homes: Local Grant can pay for insulation, a heat pump, smart controls and solar panels through your local council, with nothing for you to pay. It replaced the Home Upgrade Grant, which has closed, and it is open to homes on and off the gas grid. Ask the council before accepting an installer quote, because the council arranges the work. Households on Pension Credit and other qualifying means-tested benefits can also get the £150 Warm Home Discount off the electricity bill a heat pump runs on. Our grant checker covers everything else.

Is a heat pump worth it for a bungalow

On gas only with a heat pump tariff; on oil, clearly yes. On gas £180 a year pays back the extra £2,000 in about 11 years; on oil £4,000 at £639 a year pays back in about six.

Read the detail

On gas the case rests on the tariff. On standard electricity the heat pump costs £63 a year more to run than the gas boiler it replaces, so there is nothing to pay the installation back with. On a heat pump tariff it saves £180 a year. At the medians a three bedroom bungalow pays £5,500 after the grant against about £3,500 for a new gas boiler (the DESNZ average for a three bedroom home, including VAT), so the heat pump costs about £2,000 more, which £180 a year pays back in about 11 years, or about 14 if 5% VAT returns to electricity in April 2027. Capping the gas supply saves the £108 a year standing charge as well. The government assumes a heat pump lasts 20 years and a gas boiler 15 (Boiler Upgrade Scheme impact assessment, 2022). It is worth doing on gas when the radiators need little work, the boiler is due anyway, or you want to be off gas, and it is hard to justify on running cost alone.

On oil the picture is different and much better. With the £9,000 grant a typical three bedroom bungalow pays £4,000, and the heat pump saves £639 a year against oil on a heat pump tariff and £396 on standard electricity, at the oil price on 25 September 2026. Even if a new oil boiler would have cost nothing, £4,000 at £639 a year pays back in about six years, and every pound a new oil boiler would have cost shortens that. Oil prices move, so the saving moves with them. Run your own numbers for oil or LPG with our heat pump calculator; for gas, use our boiler vs heat pump comparison, and read heat pump vs new boiler for the 15-year view. Ground source only makes sense on a large plot: the median ground source installation under the Boiler Upgrade Scheme in 2025/26 cost £21,298 at 8 to 10 kW, against £12,686 for air source at the same size. The bungalow's big single roof is often a better use of the money, because solar panels pair well with a heat pump.

Placement, noise and simple controls

Site the unit away from the party wall, because bungalow windows sit at unit height. It must pass the 37 dB MCS 020(a) test; ask for quiet mode at night and a single zone set to 20°C.

Read the detail

On a detached bungalow, placement is the easy part: the outdoor unit goes on the side or rear wall nearest the cylinder cupboard, with nobody's window in the way. On a semi-detached bungalow the neighbour's windows decide where it goes. In England the one metre boundary rule was removed in May 2025. Permitted development now needs the MCS 020(a) sound calculation to show 37 dB or less at one metre from each neighbour's nearest habitable room window or door, a unit of no more than 1.5 cubic metres, no more than one heat pump on a house that is not detached, and a bungalow that is not listed (GPDO Schedule 2, Part 14, Class G; MCS). Wales moved to the same MCS 020(a) test on 1 June 2026 (MCS). Bungalow windows sit at the same height as the unit, with no upstairs to lift them clear, so installers site it on the far side from the party wall, facing the garden, on anti-vibration feet. At one metre the Ecodan Ultra Quiet 8.5 kW is 45 dB(A) and the Grant Aerona 290 9 kW is 46 dB(A), and distance and walls bring that down quickly; a unit forced onto the party-wall side may need a quieter model or an acoustic enclosure. Outside a conservation area the rules only stop a unit going above ground floor level on a wall facing a road, which rarely matters on a bungalow; in a conservation area it cannot go on a wall or roof facing a road at all. Our planning permission guide covers the exceptions.

Quiet mode and one zone

Many bungalow owners want a system that is quiet and needs no attention. Most units have a quiet mode that lowers the fan speed at night: Samsung's goes down to 35 dB(A), and Octopus calls its version Hush Mode. Ask for quiet mode from 11pm to 7am if the unit is near a bedroom window. Inside, ask for a single-zone setup: one floor at one temperature needs one thermostat, weather compensation set by the installer and no zone valves or timers to fail or fiddle with. Set it to 20°C and leave it on; a heat pump runs cheapest at a steady low flow temperature. If an installer offers an electric boiler as the simpler box, our electric boiler versus heat pump guide shows why it costs almost three times as much to run: £2,724 a year for this bungalow against £980 for a heat pump on standard electricity.

Getting quotes: what to check

Get three MCS quotes and compare the design, not the price. In a bungalow check the loft depth assumed, the radiator schedule, where the cylinder goes and, for oil or LPG, the £9,000 grant rate.

Read the detail

Get three quotes from MCS-certified installers and compare them on the design rather than the price. In a bungalow the loft and the cylinder are where quotes diverge. Each should include:

  • A room-by-room heat loss calculation with the design outdoor temperature and the loft depth it assumes; if you are topping up, make sure it uses 270 mm.
  • The design flow temperature, ideally 50°C or lower, and the seasonal performance expected at it.
  • A radiator schedule for every emitter, which ones change and their new sizes.
  • The cylinder size, whether it is slimline, and exactly where it goes.
  • An MCS 020(a) sound calculation with each neighbour's nearest window or door marked.
  • For oil and LPG bungalows, the £9,000 grant rate and whether tank decommissioning is included.
  • Quiet mode, a single-zone control setup, the warranties on the unit and the installation, and confirmation that the grant is already taken off the quoted price, as the scheme now requires.

Most bungalow installations take a few days, longer when several radiators are swapped or a cupboard has to be built. Find installers through the MCS installer search; oil boiler specialists who have added heat pumps know the local tanks and plots and are worth a quote alongside a heat pump specialist. Read our old house guide if the bungalow has solid walls or single glazing, and our draught-proofing guide for the cheap fixes that let a heat pump run cooler.

Ask for anti-vibration pads under the unit, whether it sits on a wall bracket or a plinth.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a heat pump cost for a bungalow?

About £12,300 for a two bedroom bungalow and £13,000 for a three bedroom one, the 2025/26 Boiler Upgrade Scheme medians for the 7.5 to 9.5 kW heat pump each needs, with most jobs costing £10,500 to £15,500. That is the whole job as installers recorded it for the grant, cylinder, labour and VAT included; some homes also need larger radiators. After the £7,500 grant a typical bungalow pays £4,800 to £5,500. Until March 2027 a bungalow heated by oil or LPG with no mains gas gets £9,000, which brings it to £3,300 to £4,000.

What size heat pump does a bungalow need?

About 7.5 kW for a typical two bedroom bungalow and 9.5 kW for a three bedroom one on our model, the same class as a 3-bed semi, because the roof and floor are each as large as the whole floor area. Well insulated bungalows need 6.5 to 8 kW and poorly insulated ones 8.5 to 10.5 kW. The installer's room-by-room heat loss calculation sets the exact size.

How much does a heat pump cost to run in a bungalow?

At the Ofgem price cap for October to December 2026, a three bedroom bungalow with 10,350 kWh of heat demand costs £737 a year on a heat pump tariff or £980 on standard electricity, against £917 for a gas boiler, £1,376 for oil at the price on 25 September 2026 and £1,157 for LPG. On a standard tariff the heat pump costs £63 a year more than gas, so the saving against gas depends on moving to a heat pump tariff. See our running costs guide.

Is a heat pump worth it for a bungalow with an oil boiler?

Usually, yes. Until March 2027 the £9,000 grant for a home heated by oil or LPG with no mains gas brings a typical three bedroom bungalow to £4,000, and on a heat pump tariff it costs £639 a year less to run than oil at the price on 25 September 2026, so even if a new oil boiler would have cost nothing the heat pump pays back in about six years. Run your own numbers for oil or LPG with our heat pump calculator.

Where does the hot water cylinder go in a bungalow?

In the airing cupboard off the hall if there is one, otherwise where the old boiler stood, in a utility room, an integral garage or a purpose-built cupboard. The Energy Saving Trust says a cylinder will usually fit a cupboard of at least 80 cm by 80 cm, and slimline models suit shallower spaces. The loft is a last resort because of the weight and the frost risk.

Do I need planning permission for a heat pump on a bungalow?

Usually not. In England it is permitted development if the MCS 020(a) sound calculation shows 37 dB or less at the neighbour's nearest habitable room window or door, the unit is no larger than 1.5 cubic metres, and the bungalow is not listed; in a conservation area it cannot go on a wall facing a road. The one metre boundary rule was removed in May 2025. See our planning permission guide.
