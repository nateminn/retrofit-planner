Our data

Every figure on this site comes from public data. Here is each source: who publishes it, what we use it for, when they last updated it, when our figures last changed because of it, and when we last checked it.

How we use it

  1. We take published data. Government statistics, the energy regulator, the Energy Saving Trust and suppliers' own price pages. Where no published figure exists, we make an assumption and list it below.
  2. We build it into one model. Heat use, energy bills, insulation costs and heat pump costs each live in one file that every calculator and guide reads, so no two pages can give different answers for the same home. Our methodology explains each step.
  3. We check every page against it. Before any change goes live, 31 automated checks compare the figures on every page with the model and the calculators, and a page that disagrees is held back.
  4. We refresh it when a source changes. The price cap every quarter, grants when the rules change, and costs when the publisher revises them.

Our own assumptions

Where there is no published figure, we say so.

  • LPG at 9.5p per kWh is our own assumption for a typical bulk contract; there is no official average.
  • The EPC estimate is a simple points model, not the government's SAP software. For a typical home of each age it lands close to the English Housing Survey 2024 average rating, but it has not been checked home by home against lodged certificates.
  • Solar generation is the PVGIS figure for the postcode district, at the centre of its homes, less 7% for shading and dirt on real roofs; the 7% is our allowance, chosen to match the cautious regional figures the calculator used before.
  • Where the Energy Saving Trust publishes no figure we borrow the nearest home: an end-terrace takes the semi-detached cost, and a top floor flat's loft takes the mid-terrace cost.
  • The typical household bill of £1,723 is the price cap rates applied to Ofgem's typical use of 2,500 kWh of electricity and 9,500 kWh of gas.
  • Heat pump efficiency by insulation: we use the trial's lower quartile (2.55) for poor insulation, its median (2.78) for average and its upper quartile (3.05) for good; 3.25 for excellent is our estimate near the top of the trial's range. The trial did not split results by insulation.
  • The heat pump tariff figure of 19.8p is our estimate for Cosy Octopus in October 2026, from its September rates; we will recheck it when Octopus publishes October rates.
  • Solar output by council averages PVGIS over up to 8 places where people live in each council; in Northern Ireland, where we have no small area centres, it uses the council's largest town.

What the figures are

Estimates for a typical home of each kind. They use the median or typical figure from each source, so your own home will differ.

A survey, a quote or an EPC assessment is what turns an estimate into a price or a rating. If a figure looks wrong or out of date, tell us and we will check it against its source.
